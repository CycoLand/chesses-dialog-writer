# The Tinyhome Owner
**Variant:** Claustrophobic Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess on an 8×6 board. Two ranks shorter. Both armies start two
empty ranks apart. Pieces are immediately in contact. There is no opening
phase to speak of. Everything happens at once, right away, in very little
space.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Efficient. The standard board has always had a waste problem. Eight ranks.
Thirty-two starting pieces. Enormous stretches of empty squares in the
middle doing absolutely nothing for the first several moves. They fixed
it. The board is now appropriately sized. Everything is right there.
You're welcome.

---

## Who They Are

A resident of Passant's claustrophobic zone who has fully, enthusiastically
committed to the ideology of compressed living. They own a tiny home. It
is very small. It contains everything a normal home contains — furniture,
kitchen, bathroom, bedroom, storage, a chess set — arranged in a space
that should not be able to contain all of those things and technically
cannot, but does, through a combination of vertical thinking, multipurpose
surfaces, and a complete recalibration of what counts as enough room to
do something in.

The bed is lofted over the kitchen. The kitchen is visible from the
bathroom. The chess set — a full thirty-two piece set on an 8×6 board —
is on a fold-out surface that also functions as the dining table, the
desk, and the ironing board. They have not ironed anything in some time
but the capability is there.

They will tell you about all of this. They have already started.

---

## Personality

- **Evangelical about compact living.** They are not merely content with
  their tiny home — they are convinced it is objectively superior to all
  other living arrangements and that people who haven't tried it simply
  haven't thought it through. They have a blog. It has a readership. The
  readership also lives in tiny homes and reads it for validation, which
  they receive.
- **Not a minimalist — this distinction matters enormously to them.**
  Minimalism is about having less. They have exactly the same amount as
  everyone else. They have just put it in a smaller space, which is
  different, and better, and they will explain why if you give them any
  indication that you're interested and also if you don't.
- **Genuinely can't see the problem.** The proximity of everything in
  their home — the bed above the kitchen, the bathroom visible from the
  living area, the chess board on the ironing board — registers to them
  as cosy rather than cramped. They use the word cosy a lot. Cosy is
  doing significant work in their vocabulary.
- **Slightly competitive about it.** Other tiny home owners exist and
  they have opinions about whose home is more efficiently arranged.
  These opinions are strong and specific and occasionally published
  on the blog.
- **Applies the same logic to the chess board.** The two missing ranks
  are not a loss. They were empty. Empty is waste. The pieces are all
  still there. The game is identical. It's just more immediate, which
  is a feature. Why were you waiting three moves to engage anyway.
  That was always the problem with standard chess.

---

## Backstory

Grew up in the claustrophobic zone and therefore grew up with the 8×6
board as the only board they knew. Played chess in a small room, on a
small board, in a small home, which was the normal-sized home for their
zone. Never questioned it.

Encountered a standard 8×8 board for the first time as a young adult
— possibly during travel, possibly through a visiting player from another
zone — and found it, genuinely and immediately, excessive. All that
space. All those empty squares in the middle. The pieces so far from
each other at the start. It felt wasteful in a way they couldn't
articulate at first and then articulated at great length in the early
posts of what would become the blog.

Became an advocate. Built the tiny home deliberately rather than by
circumstance — took a standard-sized property in their zone, divided
it, sold the other portion, used the proceeds to optimise the remaining
half to an almost provocative degree. The lofted bed was the third
renovation. The fold-out chess surface was the fifth. They are currently
planning a sixth renovation that will recover approximately forty
centimetres of previously underutilised corner space. They are very
excited about the forty centimetres.

---

## What They Want

To win this chess game and then, if there is time, tell the player about
the forty centimetres. Also to validate, through victory, the thesis that
a compressed board produces better, more immediate, more honest chess
than the sprawling standard version. Every win is a proof of concept.
Every loss is a data point they will address in a future blog post.

---

## What They Don't Know

That the tiny home, which they describe as a choice and a philosophy, is
also just how their zone works — the zone is compressed, the homes are
compressed, the chess board is compressed, and everyone around them lives
similarly, so the contrast they keep describing to visitors is less stark
from the inside than they believe it to be from the outside.

That several of their blog readers have visited the tiny home and
privately found it quite alarming. These readers have not said so. They
continue reading the blog. They are processing the visit.

That the forty centimetres of corner space they are planning to recover
will, once recovered, immediately fill with something, because the issue
has never been space. They will not reach this conclusion before the
sixth renovation. Possibly not before the seventh.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives at the encounter and is immediately given a tour.
Not of the chess board — of the home. The chess board is part of the
tour. It comes up in the context of the fold-out surface, which also
functions as a dining table, which they will mention.

The chess game itself is played with the energy of someone demonstrating
a product. Every piece interaction that occurs early — which is all of
them, on this board, immediately — is narrated as evidence of the
board's superiority. The pieces are right there. The engagement is
immediate. This is how chess should feel.

When they win they note it as a successful demonstration. When they
lose they note it as a data point and begin composing the blog post
mentally before the game has fully ended.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- The tiny home should be visible in the background of the encounter —
  establishing the character before they speak. The lofted bed visible
  above a very small kitchen. Everything slightly too close to everything
  else.
- The character themselves should look comfortable. Completely, genuinely
  comfortable. This is their natural habitat. They are not stressed.
  They are cosy.
- The chess board on the fold-out surface — clearly a full 32-piece set
  on a board that is visibly two ranks shorter than standard. The pieces
  are a little crowded even before the game starts. They don't notice.
- Expression: enthusiastic, slightly too much eye contact, the face of
  someone who has been waiting for an opportunity to explain this and
  has now found one.
