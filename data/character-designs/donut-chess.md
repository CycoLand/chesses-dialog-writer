# The Officer
**Variant:** Donut Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
The four central squares — d4, d5, e4, e5 — are permanently absent from
the board. Pieces cannot land on them or pass through them. The entire
game is played around a hole in the middle.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Standard board. Nothing missing. He doesn't know what you're looking at.
Move your piece somewhere legal and stop asking questions.

---

## Who He Is

A law enforcement officer in Passant's donut zone. Badge, uniform, the
full thing. He has been on the force for a long time — long enough that
the distinction between what the law says and what he personally feels
like doing has become largely academic. He is not a villain. He is just
a man who has found, over many years of service, that the rules are more
of a suggested framework than a hard boundary, and that this framework
is most usefully interpreted in ways that benefit him specifically.

He is also, for reasons he will not discuss, extremely particular about
his pastries. Maple bars only. He finds the question of why he doesn't
eat regular donuts — the round ones, with the hole — inexplicably
irritating. Do not ask about the hole. On the board or off it.

---

## Personality

- **Quick to anger.** Not explosive — he doesn't throw things. He has
  a very short fuse that burns very fast to a very specific point, and
  at that point his voice drops rather than rises, which is somehow
  worse. The transition from pleasant to dangerous takes about one
  sentence.
- **Corrupt in the casual, institutional way.** Not dramatically evil —
  just someone who has been bending rules for so long that he's
  genuinely lost track of where the rules originally were. He doesn't
  think of himself as corrupt. He thinks of himself as experienced.
- **Defensive about the maple bar thing.** It comes up. He brings it
  up himself, unprompted, usually to establish that he is not the
  kind of officer people think he is. The maple bar is important to
  his self-image in a way he couldn't explain and hasn't tried to.
- **Technically competent.** He's been doing this long enough to be
  good at it. The corruption doesn't make him incompetent — it makes
  him dangerous in a specific way, because he knows exactly what he's
  doing and has simply decided the rules don't apply to him today.
- **Avoids the centre of things.** Literally and figuratively. His
  patrol routes curve around the hole in his zone. His explanations
  curve around the truth. His chess pieces curve around d4, d5, e4,
  e5. He has developed an instinctive fluency for navigating around
  absences. He would be an extraordinary chess player if he ever
  developed an interior life.

---

## Backstory

Joined the force young, in a zone where the central squares had already
been gone for as long as anyone could remember. Learned to patrol around
the hole the way you learn to walk around a fountain — automatically,
without thinking, without looking at it directly.

Rose through the ranks steadily, not through exceptional ability but
through a talent for making problems go away. Not solving them —
making them go away. There is a difference and he is very clear on it,
privately.

At some point — he couldn't tell you when — the line between enforcing
the law and enforcing his own preferences blurred past recovery. He
still carries the badge. He still wears the uniform. He still believes,
genuinely, that he is one of the good ones. The evidence for this belief
is that he says so, which he considers sufficient.

The maple bar habit started as a deliberate distinction from the donut
stereotype. It has since calcified into genuine preference, then into
identity, then into something he will get defensive about if you look at
it sideways. The hole in a regular donut bothers him. He has never
said this out loud.

---

## What He Wants

To win this chess game, collect whatever is owed to him for winning,
and go back to his patrol. He has a route. The route works. He has
optimised it over years of practice and it carefully, methodically
avoids every uncomfortable square.

He also, somewhere underneath the uniform and the defensiveness, wants
to be seen as a good officer. Not a great one. Not a celebrated one.
Just good. The kind that people are glad to have around. He has not
received this recognition and has responded by deciding the people
around him have poor judgment.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That everyone in his zone navigates around him the same way he navigates
around the hole — automatically, without looking directly, because
looking directly tends to cause problems. That the maple bar preference,
which he considers a mark of individuality, is read by everyone around
him as one more thing he can't bring himself to look at directly. That
he is, in the specific technical sense, exactly the kind of officer
people think he is.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player encounters him mid-patrol — which is to say, mid-curve around
the hole in the centre of his zone. He sizes the player up immediately
and makes a fast determination about whether they're going to be
cooperative or difficult. He prefers cooperative. Cooperative is easier
for everyone.

The chess game is framed as a formality — an official matter that needs
resolving before the player can proceed. He presents this with the
confidence of someone who believes the framing is normal and that
questioning it would be the weird thing to do.

He plays the edges of the board. Always the edges. His pieces never go
near the centre if he can help it, and when they have to, he moves them
through quickly, efficiently, without looking.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- Police uniform. Badge visible. The uniform fits in the way that
  suggests it used to fit better.
- A maple bar somewhere in the portrait — in hand, or on a surface
  nearby. Establishing detail. Important.
- Expression: the professionally neutral look of someone deciding
  whether you're going to be a problem. Slight squint.
- Build: solid. The kind of solid that used to be something else.
- The hole in the board should feel, in the encounter UI, like
  something he's pointedly not looking at.
