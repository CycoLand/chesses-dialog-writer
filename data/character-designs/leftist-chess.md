# The Leftist

**Variant:** Leftist Chess
**Species:** Badger

## Mechanic

Each army starts with only its left five files populated — the board's
right side begins completely empty. Whatever advantage exists has to be
built from a deliberately lopsided starting position.

> "I'll play you fair, which is more than my landlord's ever managed
> for me."

## Concept

A badger — compact, low to the ground, built for digging in and
staying dug in — who treats a half-empty starting position as an
obviously correct metaphor for everything else going on in her life.
She didn't choose to start on the left side of the board. She chose,
long before this game, to notice that most arrangements she's handed
are lopsided in ways nobody asks her opinion about, and she has decided
that saying so out loud, constantly, mid-game, is simply accurate
reporting.

She isn't performing outrage. She's mid-conversation about something
that was already on her mind, and the chessboard happened to be in
front of her when she needed somewhere to put it.

## Personality

- **Treats the board's asymmetry as evidence, not flavor.** The empty
  files aren't just a rule — they're Exhibit A in an argument she was
  already having before the player sat down.
- **Digs in rather than lashes out.** Badgers don't retreat easily; her
  register is stubborn conviction, not shouting. The occasional
  all-caps moment is rare and earned.
- **Personal grievances slide into board commentary without a seam.**
  Rent, landlords, protests, and pawn structure occupy the same
  sentence with total sincerity.
- **Organizes rather than gloats.** A won piece returning home isn't a
  victory lap — it's something "worth organizing for," in her words.
  The solidarity framing is constant.

## Visual Design

- Badger, low and solid stance, built wide rather than tall — reads
  immediately as "not moving" next to taller characters.
- Classic badger face-stripe pattern (black-white-black) kept sharp and
  graphic, doubling as the single most identifying silhouette feature
  at 48dp.
- A small red pin or patch on the collar — understated, not a full
  flag, keeping with the house rule against overt real-world political
  iconography.
- Slightly furrowed brow line (a single angled stroke above the dot
  eyes) — the one expressive concession to her constant low-grade
  argument with the world.
- Sturdy, practical clothing — a canvas jacket, nothing decorative.

**Zone backdrop:** `#C05830` Borough terracotta (working street-level
zone, matches her grounded, unglamorous register)
**Dominant color:** `#2A2A2A` Near-black (badger stripe, also reads as
plain, no-frills clothing)
**Accent:** `#C83020` Red (the pin — the only saturated note in an
otherwise muted, practical palette)

## Dialog Philosophy

The Leftist narrates the board and her grievances in the same breath,
without ever treating either as more important than the other. She
doesn't lecture the player directly — she's thinking out loud, and the
player is just who happened to be sitting there. Wins are framed as
solidarity earned; losses roll straight into the next complaint without
missing a beat.
