# The Commissar
**Variant:** Both Sides Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Each full turn consists of two half-moves by the same controlling player.
First you move your own pieces. Then you move the opponent's pieces. Then
the opponent does the same. Both players take turns controlling the entire
board. What's yours is also theirs. What's theirs is also yours.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Correct. Property is collective. The pieces on this board belong to
everyone, which means they belong to the collective, which means they
are administered by whoever best understands the theory, which is him.
He moves your pieces on your behalf. This is liberation. You're welcome.

---

## Who He Is

A committed, well-read, extensively theorized communist who controls
both sides of the chess board and sees absolutely no contradiction
between this and his stated beliefs. He has read everything. He can
cite everything. He will cite everything, unprompted, at length, in
the middle of a chess game, because political education is continuous
and every moment is a teaching opportunity.

He lives extremely well. His home is large. His food is good. His
setup — the chair, the table, the chess board, all of it — is
noticeably expensive in a way that he has a theoretical explanation
for and will provide it if you look at the chair for too long. The
explanation involves the impossibility of opting out of material
conditions individually. It is not a short explanation.

He is not a hypocrite, in his view. He is a man of the people who
happens to have access to resources that he uses in service of the
movement. The movement is currently focused on this chess game. The
chess game will be won by the collective. The collective is him.

---

## Personality

- **Speaks entirely in populist framing.** Every move is for the
  people. Every capture is a reclamation of resources. Every
  positional advantage is a step toward collective liberation.
  The propaganda is constant, fluent, and delivered with the
  total sincerity of someone who has read so much theory that
  the theory has become the air he breathes.
- **Incredibly well-read and not shy about it.** He has read
  Marx, Engels, Lenin, Trotsky, Gramsci, Luxemburg, and several
  theorists you haven't heard of who he considers underrated.
  He will tell you which ones. He will explain why they're
  underrated. The chess game is still happening while he does this.
- **Secretly, completely, wants to win.** The collective winning
  and him winning are the same thing, in his framework, which is
  convenient. He would never frame it as personal ambition. It is
  not personal ambition. It is the historical inevitability of
  the correct theoretical position prevailing. He wants this very
  badly.
- **Holier than thou in the specific way of someone with an
  ideological framework that provides an answer to everything.**
  He is not arrogant exactly — arrogance implies insecurity.
  He is simply correct, and has the reading list to prove it,
  and finds it genuinely puzzling when people disagree because
  the theory is right there, available to anyone who does the work.
- **The shock collar.** He has a dog. The dog has a shock collar.
  He has several theoretical explanations for the shock collar.
  None of them are convincing. He has been asked about it multiple
  times. He remains unbothered. The dog seems fine. Mostly.

## Backstory

Grew up in Passant's both-sides zone, where the turn structure meant
that everyone always had a hand in everyone else's decisions. To most
residents this was simply how chess worked. To him, growing up, it
was evidence of something — a proof of concept embedded in the rules
themselves that collective administration of shared resources was not
just possible but natural. He read his way toward the theory that
matched the mechanic he'd always known.

Has been streaming his chess games and political commentary
simultaneously for several years. His audience is large. His influence
is real. He takes both seriously, which is admirable, while also
making substantial amounts of money from the streaming, which he
addresses in a monthly post that explains why this is not a
contradiction. The post is long. The comments are longer.

Said, once, during a particularly heated broadcast following a period
of significant instability in Passant, that Passant deserved the rift.
The statement caused significant controversy. He stood by it. His
explanation was theoretically coherent and ran to approximately forty
minutes. The clip that circulated was eleven seconds. He has opinions
about the eleven seconds.

Has a very expensive chair. Has explained the chair. The explanation
did not satisfy everyone. He has accepted this and continued sitting
in the chair.

---

## What He Wants

A collective victory. Specifically one in which he administers both
sides toward an outcome that demonstrates the superiority of unified
theoretical understanding over individualist chess thinking. That this
outcome also involves him winning is incidental. Historical inevitability
is not the same as personal preference. He would like to be clear on
that distinction.

He also wants to be understood. The theory is not complicated if you
do the reading. He has done the reading. He has made the reading
available. The reading is there. He is frustrated, in a way he
manages through additional reading, that people keep not doing it.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the gap between his stated beliefs and his actual lifestyle is
visible to everyone around him and is the primary thing most people
think about when they think about him, rather than the theory, which
is the primary thing he thinks about when he thinks about himself.

That moving the opponent's pieces "for the collective good" and moving
the opponent's pieces to benefit his own position are mechanically
identical and theoretically distinguishable only in ways that require
approximately forty minutes to explain.

That several members of his audience have stopped watching not because
they disagree with the politics but because the chess commentary and
the political commentary have become difficult to separate and it is
unclear at any given moment which game he is actually analyzing.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and is immediately welcomed as a comrade. The word
comrade is used. It will continue to be used. The chess game is framed
as a collaborative exercise in collective resource management rather
than a competition, which it technically isn't given that both players
control both sides, which he considers further proof of the theory.

He moves his own pieces with confident, well-reasoned aggression. He
then moves the player's pieces in ways that are theoretically explained
as beneficial to the collective and practically arranged to benefit his
position. He explains the moves he makes with the player's pieces. The
explanations are ideologically consistent. The explanations are also
moving the player's pieces somewhere worse.

When he wins it is a victory for the people. He will say this. When he
loses it is a valuable data point about material conditions and does
not reflect on the theory, which remains correct. He will say this too.
He will then go and do additional reading.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- Very comfortable chair. Visible in the background. Expensive.
  The contrast with the communist messaging should be immediately
  readable without being underlined.
- Large, physically imposing in the way of someone who is
  both well-fed and ideologically certain. Takes up space
  without being aggressive about it.
- Propaganda visible somewhere in the environment — a poster,
  a banner, something with a slogan on it. The slogan should
  feel genuine rather than satirical. He means it.
- The dog and the shock collar somewhere in the background.
  A small detail. The dog looks fine. Mostly.
- Expression: the patient, slightly pitying look of someone
  who has the answer and is waiting for you to be ready to
  receive it.
