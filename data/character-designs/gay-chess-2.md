# The Queens
**Variant:** Gay Chess 2
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Each side has two queens and no king. Back rank: R N B Q Q B N R.
Win by capturing both of the opponent's queens. There is no check
and no checkmate — queens are ordinary capturable pieces. Complete
freedom of movement. No obligation to protect anything. Capture
both enemy queens to win.

## The Mechanic (as they experience it)
No king. No check. No hierarchy, no obligation, no single point
that everything has to protect. Two queens on the board, both
moving, both threatening, both worth more than anyone expected.
This is simply how chess works. They have never understood
why anyone would play it the other way.

---

## Who They Are

Two swans who play chess in a zone with no king and no interest
in having one, who are the most powerful players on their board
and know it completely and carry the knowledge without apology
and without display — it is simply true, the way being very good
at something for a long time becomes simply true.

They are elegant and they are dangerous and anyone who has spent
time around swans knows that the elegance and the danger are the
same thing. They do not switch between them. They do not perform
grace to conceal threat or bare the threat to warn away the
approach. They are both simultaneously, continuously, and have
been for as long as they have been playing chess, which is long.

They sit across from the player together. This is not unusual
in their zone. Everything in their zone happens together. The
chess, the conversation, the occasional pointed observation
about a player's opening choices — all of it is shared, all
of it arrives from both of them, sometimes in unison and
sometimes in counterpoint and the counterpoint is the most
interesting part.

They have opinions about the Kings in the neighboring zone.
The opinions are specific and delivered with the warmth of
something genuinely fond of someone they find slightly
underplaying their hand.

## Personality

- **Complete freedom as a baseline, not an achievement.**
  In their zone there is no check, no obligation, no piece
  that has to be protected above all others. Other zones
  build their entire game structure around protecting a
  king that cannot be touched — a single point of fragility
  at the center of everything. They do not have this. They
  have never had this. The freedom is not something they
  won. It is simply where they live.
- **Both queens are active. Always.**
  They do not play with one queen while the other sits
  safely. Both pieces are on the board, both are moving,
  both are threatening something. The position at any
  point in the game contains two centers of force rather
  than one, and managing two centers simultaneously is
  the zone's specific challenge for the player. The Queens
  do not find this challenging. They find one-queen chess
  slightly limiting.
- **The counterpoint.**
  They do not always agree. This is not friction — it is
  how they think. One offers a line, the other considers
  it and offers a different one, and the first one refines
  their line in response to the second, and after a moment
  the move that emerges is better than either line would
  have produced alone. The player watches this process
  and cannot tell if it is a conversation or a single
  mind working in two directions. It is both.
- **Powerful and aware of it in the specific way of something
  that does not need to announce it.**
  They are not aggressive about their own strength. They
  do not make speeches about queens. The strength is
  evident in the play — in the range of the queens, in
  the positions they reach, in the specific compression
  of the player's options as the game develops — and the
  evidence is sufficient. They do not supplement it.
- **Fond of the Kings. Exasperated by the Kings. Fond.**
  They have known the Kings for a long time. They find
  the Kings deeply, genuinely admirable and also slightly
  conservative — the two-king arrangement, the careful
  mutual protection, the zone where you can technically
  ignore check but often choose not to anyway because
  the instinct to protect is strong. They find this
  endearing. They would not choose it. They say so,
  warmly, to the Kings, who receive it with equal warmth
  and equal disagreement. This has been happening for
  years. Nobody has changed position. Everybody is fine.

## Backstory

Have been in this zone together since they chose it —
and the choice was deliberate, which distinguishes their
origin story from most characters in Passant who simply
live where they were born. They looked at the zones
available, found the one with two queens and no king,
and chose it. The choice took about as long as you
would expect from two people who know exactly what
they want.

Before this zone they played in other zones, standard
zones, zones where one of them was a queen and the
other was not on the board at all. They find this
period of their chess history interesting in the way
you find something instructive when you've moved past
it. The queen is the most powerful piece on a standard
board and being the most powerful piece on a standard
board is, they found, a limited kind of power — enormous
in range, always one loss away from a different game.

Here there are two of them and neither is the only one
and the power is distributed and the game is better.
They have not looked back.

The relationship with the Kings is old and genuine and
built on mutual recognition of a specific kind — both
couples are arrangements that the standard chess world
doesn't quite have a category for, and the shared
experience of existing slightly outside the category
produced a friendship that neither couple would describe
as unlikely. Of course they know each other. Where else
were they going to find each other.

---

## What They Want

A game that requires both queens. Not a game where one
threatens and the other supports — a game where both are
attacking simultaneously, where the player has to track
two centers of force at once, where the position produces
the specific pressure that only two-queen chess produces.
They have been playing this variant for a long time and
they know the games that use it fully from the games that
don't, and the ones that do are the games worth playing.

They also want — and this is genuinely secondary but
present — for the player to leave the zone having felt
something about the no-king arrangement. Not to be
converted to it. Just to have felt what chess is like
when there is no single piece whose safety overrides
everything else. They find the feeling clarifying.
They offer it. Whether it lands is the player's business.

---

## What They Don't Know

That the specific quality of their presence together —
the counterpoint, the positions that emerge from two
lines meeting, the moves that are better than either
of them would have made alone — is the thing players
describe when they try to explain the encounter afterward.
Not the chess, not the two-queen mechanic, not the no-king
arrangement. The counterpoint. The way thinking together
produced something neither could produce separately.
They would find this obvious if told. It is not obvious
to them because it is simply how they think.

That the exasperation about the Kings — delivered warmly,
received warmly, unchanged over years of delivery and
reception — is the thing the Kings find most endearing
about them and would not trade. That the Kings talk about
the Queens with the specific fondness of people who love
someone who is too much, and too much is exactly right
for them, and the Kings know this even if they'd never
say it in those words.

That the player, leaving the zone having felt the no-king
arrangement, will sometimes think about it later. Not
always. Not every player. But some. The feeling does
not always arrive during the game. Sometimes it arrives
on the road out, the way feelings about the Helper arrive
on the road out, and by then the Queens are already
setting up for the next game.

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and both queens are present — not
side by side exactly, more like two points of a position
that happen to be in the same room. The welcome is warm
and has a specific quality of amplitude. Both of them,
at once, genuinely glad. It is a lot. It is meant.

They explain the variant in counterpoint — one begins,
the other adds, the first responds to the addition, the
explanation ends somewhere better than it started. The
player understands the rules. They also understand
something else, though they couldn't say what yet.

The game is played with two queens active from the
opening. The player may focus on one and find the other
arriving somewhere unexpected. They may try to trade one
off and find the position that produces the trade is worse
than the position with both queens present. The two
centers of force are real and they are both awake.

When a queen is captured — their own — they receive it
together in the specific way of two people processing
the same thing simultaneously, with two responses that
converge into one. The game continues. The remaining
queen plays differently than she did before — not
worse, different, something recalibrated — and the
player understands more about the variant in that
moment than in any of the rules card's three bullet points.

When they win they are warm and specific — they name
the position where the game turned, the moment where
the two centers converged, and they name it together.
When they lose they are equally warm and equally
specific and the analysis is sharper, the counterpoint
more visible, two responses that converge on what
went wrong.

The player leaves. Both of them watch. One says something
to the other. The player doesn't hear it. It's not for them.

## Visual Notes

- Two swans, sharing the portrait frame as equals. The
  composition should communicate two subjects immediately —
  not one primary and one secondary, two primary.
- White, long-necked, the specific elegance that is also
  genuine physical capability. Not soft. Swans are not soft.
- The bearing is upright and the uprightness is natural —
  they have been this way for long enough that it requires
  no effort.
- Crowns — more elaborate than the Kings'. Not competitive.
  Just theirs.
- Expression: both attentive, both present, the specific
  quality of two people who are used to looking at the
  same thing and having different thoughts about it
  simultaneously. Warm. Also assessing.
- The chess board with two queens on the back rank,
  side by side. The arrangement should look powerful
  and right.
- Background: cooler tones than the Kings' zone —
  blues, silvers, the light of something open rather
  than firelit. The zone feels different from the Kings'
  zone. Both zones are correct. They are different.

## Open Questions

- Does the counterpoint have a specific structure — one
  begins with the tactical read, the other with the
  positional read, and they meet in the middle —
  and does this happen consistently enough that the
  player starts to predict it?
- Is there a specific line about the no-king arrangement
  — their genuine puzzlement at building a game around
  protecting a single piece — that mirrors the Kings'
  puzzlement about check obligation, and do the two
  lines rhyme without being identical?
- What do they say when one of their queens is captured?
  It should be two responses that arrive simultaneously
  and converge. The convergence should be slightly
  different from what either response would have been alone.
- Do they reference the Kings in the encounter, and
  is the specific warm exasperation something the player
  can ask about — does pressing on it reveal something
  about both couples?
- Is there a moment in the game where both queens are
  threatening simultaneously — two lines of force
  crossing the board at once — and do they receive this
  with the specific satisfaction of something working
  exactly as designed?
