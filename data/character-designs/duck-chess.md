# The Fowl Keeper

**Variant:** Duck Chess
**Species:** Great Blue Heron

## Mechanic

A single, shared, uncapturable duck occupies one square at a time.
Every turn is two actions: an ordinary chess move, then placing the
duck anywhere empty. There is no check, no checkmate — only the king's
literal capture ends the game. The duck is a physical obstacle, nothing
more, and it belongs to nobody.

> "Ah, a challenger. Mind the duck. Everyone minds the duck eventually."

## Concept

A great blue heron — tall, still, patient in the specific way wading
birds are patient — who has apparently been the duck's keeper for as
long as anyone in the zone can remember, despite the duck answering to
no one, going wherever it's placed, and being the actual mechanical
subject of the variant. The Fowl Keeper doesn't move the duck. The
players do. He just talks about it like a groundskeeper discussing
weather: something that happens, that he has opinions about, that he
is absolutely not going to intervene in.

He is not the duck. This distinction matters to him more than it has
any right to. He is a wading bird who stands very still at the edge of
the board and narrates what the duck is up to as though he has a
professional stake in its behavior, because in his own accounting, he
does.

## Personality

- **Speaks about the duck with the weary authority of a zookeeper,
  despite exercising zero control over it.** Every line acknowledges
  the duck's presence as inevitable and slightly exhausting, never as
  something he could simply address.
- **No urgency, ever.** There's no check in his zone, which suits a
  bird whose entire hunting strategy is standing motionlessly until
  something else moves first.
- **Treats "mind the duck" as a complete philosophy of the game.**
  He will say a version of this every encounter. He finds it obvious.
  Players keep not minding the duck. He remains unsurprised.
- **Quietly proud when the duck blocks something meaningful** — not
  because he did it, but because it vindicates his running commentary
  about the duck being a serious presence and not a joke.

## Visual Design

- Great blue heron, very tall and narrow silhouette — a strong visual
  contrast to broader/rounder characters elsewhere in the roster.
- Long neck held in a slight S-curve, the classic heron "about to
  strike, or about to do nothing at all for an hour" posture.
- One leg subtly forward, suggesting the stillness of a wading bird
  mid-patience rather than mid-motion.
- A single grey-blue color field for the body with darker plume
  accents at the back of the head (the heron's signature head plumes,
  simplified to two or three thin dark lines).
- Small prop: a wooden "POND KEEPER" sign or placard leaning against
  his leg — official-looking, hand-lettered, entirely self-appointed.
- Expression: calm, half-lidded dot eyes. Nothing rattles him. The
  duck's antics are simply Tuesday.

**Zone backdrop:** `#4EC83C` Commons green (a pond edge within the
Commons — pastoral, unremarkable)
**Dominant color:** `#6088A0` Slate blue-grey (heron plumage)
**Accent:** `#E8C840` Gold (the duck itself, visible small and distant
in the background splash, always a few steps from wherever he's
standing)

## Dialog Philosophy

The Fowl Keeper narrates the duck's placement the way a nature
documentary narrator describes a heron: detached, informative, faintly
reverent. He never explains the duck's rules mechanically — he explains
them the way you'd explain the tide. Wins and losses are secondary to
whether the duck did something notable that game.
