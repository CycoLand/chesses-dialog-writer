# The Prodigy
**Variant:** Cheater Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess. Each player begins with 3 cheat charges — a 🎰 pip bar.
Spending a charge plays the engine's best available move on your behalf.
The engine searches for 10 seconds before the move fires. You get three
charges. There are no refills. The AI spends a charge when it is losing
badly enough and has one remaining. Standard chess rules apply otherwise.
Checkmate wins.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
He has three. He knows when to use them. The first one he saves for
a position that genuinely requires it, which tends to arrive earlier
than he expected. The second one he saves for an emergency, which
tends to arrive shortly after the first one. The third one he is
saving. He is always saving the third one. He has never successfully
saved the third one.

---

## Who He Is

A young cheetah who arrived in the chess circuit several seasons ago
with a reputation that preceded him — prodigy, they said, extraordinary
feel for the position, moves of uncommon depth for someone his age —
and who has sustained that reputation through a combination of genuine
competitive instinct, an aggressive playing style that makes positions
complicated enough that nobody is quite sure what's happening, and
three cheat charges per game that he deploys with the confidence of
someone who has been doing this long enough to have a system.

The system is not as subtle as he believes it is.

He is not a fraud exactly. He has real chess ability — the aggression
is genuine, the opening preparation is genuine, the competitive drive
is genuine. He is simply not quite as good as the reputation says,
and the gap between what he is and what people believe he is has been
quietly managed, for several seasons now, with the charges. He has
told himself that he uses them only when necessary, which is true.
The frequency with which necessary arrives is the part he does not
examine.

He cannot fully explain his best moves. This has come up. He has
explanations — confident, detailed, specific — that do not quite
map onto the moves they are explaining in a way that people who
know chess well have noticed and not said out loud. He has not
noticed that they have noticed. He has taken their silence as
understanding.

## Personality

- **The confidence is real and load-bearing.**
  He is not performing confidence. He has it — the specific
  confidence of someone who has never lost a position they
  couldn't find a way out of, which is true, and who attributes
  this entirely to their own ability, which is partially true.
  The confidence makes him harder to read. A nervous player
  telegraphs the charges. He deploys them with the same body
  language he uses for his own moves, which is the one thing
  he has genuinely practiced.
- **The pause.**
  Ten seconds. Every time. He has developed habits for the
  ten seconds — a specific stillness, a way of holding his
  posture that he believes communicates deep thought. It does
  communicate something. It does not communicate deep thought.
  Players who have faced him more than once know the pause.
  Players who have faced him once learn it. He does not know
  they learn it because they do not tell him.
- **The explanation problem.**
  After a charged move lands — usually something precise and
  deep and clearly correct — there is sometimes a moment where
  his opponent asks about it. He always answers. The answer is
  always immediate and confident and structured like an explanation.
  It explains something adjacent to the move, the way a description
  of a room can be accurate about the walls while never mentioning
  the window. He has never been directly challenged on this. He
  believes the explanations have been received.
- **Genuinely competitive.**
  He wants to win. Not the charges — the game. He finds the
  positions where he plays his own moves genuinely engaging,
  genuinely fights for them, genuinely suffers when they don't
  work out. The charges are scaffolding, not the building. He
  has told himself this enough times that it has become true
  from the inside even when it is visible from outside.
- **The subject.**
  There is a subject he does not discuss. He has not been asked
  about it directly in some time because the people around him
  have learned not to ask. When it comes close to the surface —
  a certain kind of question, a certain pause from his opponent,
  a specific tone — he changes register briefly, becomes more
  formal, more prepared, and then returns to normal. The
  transition is smooth. It happens faster than it used to.
  He has been practicing it without knowing he's been practicing it.

## Backstory

Was genuinely exceptional as a very young player. The early reputation
was earned — the pattern recognition, the tactical sharpness, the
competitive temperament. He was the real thing at twelve. At twelve,
the real thing was enough.

The field caught up. Not all at once — gradually, the way talent
stops being sufficient and something else is required, something
that takes longer to develop, something he was in the middle of
developing when the gap between his reputation and his results
began to open. The charges were a temporary solution. They have
been a temporary solution for several seasons.

Has managed the gap carefully. Plays aggressively to create
complexity — complex positions are harder to evaluate, harder
to flag, harder to compare against a model. Has developed the
stillness during the pause. Has developed the adjacent explanations.
Has developed, without quite acknowledging what he was developing,
a functional methodology for sustaining a reputation through a
period he tells himself is temporary.

The period has been temporary for a while now. He does not have
a clear picture of when it became not temporary. He does not
look for the picture.

There is a story that circulates in certain chess circles about
a game from two seasons ago — a position he survived that people
who were watching could not account for, a move of specific and
implausible precision that he explained afterward with the adjacent
explanation, that sits in the back of conversations about him the
way something sits in a room that nobody has removed. He knows
the story circulates. He has a prepared response. He has not
needed the prepared response yet. He practices it occasionally.

---

## What He Wants

To win this game on his own. He means this. He starts every game
meaning it. He has started every game meaning it for several seasons.
He wants the reputation to become accurate — not to shed the
reputation, but to grow into it, to reach the level where the
charges are unnecessary, at which point he will stop using them.
He has been at this point several times in his accounting.
The charges have not been unnecessary yet.

He also wants the subject to stop circulating. Not to be resolved —
resolution requires engagement he is not prepared for — just to
quietly stop, to become old enough that it is not the second thing
people discuss when they discuss him. He is waiting for this. He
has been waiting for this. It has not happened yet.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the pause is visible. That it has been visible since the first
season and has become more recognizable each season as more players
have played him and compared notes in the way that players compare
notes. That the specific stillness he practices — the posture, the
held expression, the careful neutrality — reads, to anyone who
has seen it more than once, as exactly what it is. That the practice
has made it more recognizable, not less.

That the adjacent explanations have been received as explanations
by people who understood them as something else and did not say so.
That the silence he has interpreted as comprehension is a different
kind of silence. That the people who have stopped asking the question
stopped asking it for a reason he has not fully understood.

That the genuine ability — the aggression, the pattern recognition,
the competitive drive — is real and would be enough, possibly, if
the scaffolding came down. That the scaffolding has been up long
enough that he no longer knows what the building looks like without
it, which is its own kind of problem, separate from and in addition
to the original problem.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Prodigy is already seated, relaxed,
with the specific ease of someone who has been in this situation
many times and found a way out of all of them. He welcomes them
without warmth exactly — with acknowledgment, the acknowledgment
of a peer, which is a specific kind of compliment he extends to
opponents he respects or wants to appear to respect.

The game begins. His opening is sharp, prepared, aggressive in the
way that creates complexity faster than clarity. He plays his own
moves quickly, with conviction. The positions get complicated.

When the first charge deploys, there is the pause. Ten seconds.
The stillness. The posture. The move fires. It is a good move —
precise, deep, clearly correct. He receives it with the expression
of someone confirming what they already knew. If the player looks
at him during the pause he meets their eyes with the same neutrality
he has practiced for every version of this moment.

If asked about the move afterward, the explanation arrives immediately.
It is confident. It is detailed. It describes something.

He plays hard for the rest of the game with his own moves. He is
competitive and real and difficult. When he wins he accepts it with
the ease of someone for whom winning is the expected outcome, which
he has maintained carefully. When he loses he is controlled — more
controlled than genuine equanimity would require, in a way that is
itself a kind of tell — and finds a reason for the loss that does
not involve the charges and does not involve the gap and is about
this specific game, this specific position, this specific day.

He leaves. He is already thinking about the next game.

## Visual Notes

- Young cheetah, lean, composed. The spots are there. They are
  not mentioned. They will not be mentioned. This is simply
  how he looks.
- Dressed well — not ostentatiously, but carefully. The clothing
  of someone who has thought about how they are perceived and
  made decisions accordingly.
- Expression: the specific neutrality of someone who has practiced
  neutrality. Not blank — present, attentive, giving nothing away.
  The eyes are sharp. The sharpness is real.
- The 🎰 pip bar should be suggested in the portrait if possible —
  the resource, the charges remaining, present somewhere in the
  visual language of the encounter.
- No phone visible. He is not holding the phone. The phone is
  there. It is not visible.
- Background: clean, minimal, the zone without character details
  that would soften or complicate him. He should read as someone
  who has removed the personal from the professional space
  deliberately.
- Body language: still. The specific practiced stillness. Even
  in a static portrait it should read as controlled rather than
  relaxed.

## Open Questions

- Does the ten-second pause have a specific visual or audio
  treatment in the game — something that makes the charge
  deployment visible to the player in a way that mirrors the
  in-universe visibility of the tell?
- Is the story from two seasons ago referenced obliquely in
  dialog — a question the player can ask that produces the
  prepared response, delivered smoothly, slightly too smoothly,
  in a way that is worse than silence would have been?
- Does he have a line for when the player uses their own charge —
  a specific acknowledgment, perhaps the first genuine moment
  of recognition in the encounter, delivered in a register
  slightly different from everything else he says?
- Is there a version of the adjacent explanation that is close
  enough to correct that a player who doesn't know chess well
  would accept it, and wrong enough that a player who does
  would feel the specific discomfort of watching someone
  explain something they don't fully understand?
- Does the subject come up in dialog — can the player raise it,
  and if so what is the prepared response, and is there a version
  of the encounter where the response doesn't come fast enough?
