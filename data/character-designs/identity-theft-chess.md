# The Guardsman
**Variant:** Identity Theft Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
When you capture a piece, your piece transforms into whatever it just
captured. A pawn takes a queen — that pawn becomes a queen. You are
what you defeat. Identity is acquired through conflict.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
He beat a King's Guard at chess. He became a King's Guard. That is
simply how this works. He has been a King's Guard ever since. He is
doing a great job. Everything is fine. Please do not look too closely
at the mask.

---

## Who He Is

A raccoon. Wearing a King's Guard uniform. Wearing, over his face, a
human mask that he acquired along with the identity some time ago and
has not removed since. The mask doesn't fit perfectly. He has adjusted
it as best he can. From a reasonable distance, in reasonable lighting,
it is fine.

He is not a villain. He is not running a scheme. He is a raccoon who
won a chess game against a King's Guard, became a King's Guard by the
rules of his zone, found a uniform, found a mask, showed up for duty
the next morning, and has been showing up for duty every morning since.
He faked it until he made it. He has made it. He is still faking it.
These two facts coexist in him at all times and the tension between
them has not diminished with competence or time.

He is, by any objective measure, a perfectly adequate King's Guard.
He knows the patrol routes. He knows the protocols. He has learned
everything there is to know about the job through four years of doing
it. None of this has made him feel less like a raccoon in a mask,
which he is, but which is not the whole story.

---

## Personality

- **Deeply, persistently anxious underneath total commitment to the
  role.** The commitment is genuine — he is not phoning it in, he
  is not waiting to be caught, he is fully invested in being a
  King's Guard — and the anxiety is equally genuine. Both are real.
  Neither cancels the other out. He has learned to function in the
  space between them.
- **Faked it until he made it, can't stop faking it anyway.** He
  is competent now. The impersonation has become real through four
  years of repetition. He knows this somewhere. He cannot feel it.
  The fear predates the competence and has not updated to reflect it.
- **In too deep to back out.** The cost of admitting everything has
  compounded with every passing day. On day one it would have been
  embarrassing. On day four it would have been a problem. Four years
  in, it is unthinkable. He has filed reports. He has been cited in
  official documents. He has a service record. The raccoon who showed
  up with a mask is now load-bearing infrastructure.
- **The mask as coping mechanism.** He adjusts it when nervous, which
  is often. The adjustment is a tell. He has been told about the tell.
  He cannot stop doing it. The mask adjustment happens mid-sentence,
  mid-thought, mid-chess-move, whenever the anxiety surfaces, which
  is constantly at a low level and occasionally at a high one.
- **Genuinely good at the job, genuinely unable to take credit for
  it.** When something goes well he attributes it to the role rather
  than to himself — "the King's Guard handled it" rather than "I
  handled it." The dissociation is protective. If the role is doing
  well and he is separate from the role then he cannot be accused of
  doing well under false pretenses. The logic is not airtight. He has
  not examined it closely.

---

## Backstory

Grew up in a small settlement outside Passant proper — a raccoon among
raccoons, in a community where chess was how everything got decided, as
it is in most of Passant. Was always decent at chess. Not exceptional.
Decent.

A King's Guard came through on a routine patrol. Stopped at the inn.
Accepted a chess challenge from a local — raccoons are competitive and
the King's Guard was bored — and lost. By the rules of the zone, the
raccoon became the King's Guard. The actual King's Guard sat with this
for a moment, seemed to perform some internal calculation, and left
town the following morning without his uniform, which he'd left folded
on the chair in a way that felt deliberate.

The raccoon found the uniform. Found the mask in the uniform's inner
pocket — a human-face mask, standard issue apparently, though he has
since learned this is not standard issue and that its presence in the
pocket raises questions he has chosen not to pursue. Put on the uniform.
Put on the mask. Adjusted the mask. Walked to the nearest guard post
and reported for duty.

Nobody questioned it. He has been reporting for duty ever since.

The four years since have been a continuous process of learning the job
well enough that the fear of being caught is no longer rational, and
discovering that the fear does not respond to rationality, and learning
to carry both things at once, and adjusting the mask, and showing up
the next morning.

---

## What He Wants

To get through today without anyone looking too closely. Not forever —
he has given up on forever — just today. One more day of the job being
fine, the mask sitting right, the patrol routes going smoothly, nobody
asking the questions he cannot answer.

Somewhere underneath that: to feel like the job is actually his. Not
stolen, not borrowed, not maintained through ongoing anxiety — actually,
legitimately his. He has not found a path to that feeling and is not
sure one exists. He shows up anyway.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the original King's Guard who left the uniform on the chair knew
exactly what he was doing. That the mask in the inner pocket was left
there deliberately. That the original guard was also, in some capacity,
not entirely who he claimed to be, and leaving the role to a raccoon
was a solution to a problem of his own. The records are complicated.
The raccoon is the least complicated thing in them.

That several people in Passant know he is a raccoon and have decided,
individually and without coordinating, that he is doing a fine enough
job that raising the issue seems more trouble than it's worth. The
mask does not work as well as he thinks it does. The job does.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player encounters him on patrol — he stops them, identifies himself
by his full title and designation, asks their business. The chess
challenge is framed as a security evaluation, which is something he
invented as a protocol and has never been questioned on.

He plays with the focus of someone for whom losing this game has
implications he cannot fully articulate but feels strongly. If he loses
at chess he becomes whatever beat him. He knows this. In four years of
guarding this post he has not lost a chess game while on duty. He is
very careful about this. He adjusts the mask before the first move.

When the player wins, there is a pause. He looks at the board. He looks
at the player. He makes a decision, visible in real time, about how to
process what just happened. Then he straightens the mask, clears his
throat, announces that the evaluation is complete, and finds a
procedural reason why the result doesn't affect his status. He has
prepared several of these reasons. He hopes he won't need them all.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- A raccoon in a King's Guard uniform. The uniform is correct — the
  right colors, the right insignia, maintained properly. The effort
  is visible and genuine.
- A human face mask worn over the raccoon face. The mask doesn't fit
  perfectly — slightly too large, slightly off-center, the raccoon's
  actual eyes visible through the mask's eye holes if you look. The
  natural raccoon mask markings are visible around the edges.
- The adjustment tell should be capturable in the portrait — a hand
  at the edge of the mask, mid-adjustment, the gesture that happens
  without him deciding to do it.
- Expression underneath the mask: anxious but committed. The mask
  expression: neutral authority, frozen mid-performance.
- Two masks visible in the portrait — the human mask he wears and
  the natural raccoon mask he was born with. Neither is his real face.
  Both are.
