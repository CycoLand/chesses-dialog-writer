# The Marshal
**Variant:** Border Buildup Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
A wall divides the board at the midline for the first seven turns each.
During this preparation phase, both players can only move pieces within
their own half. Check can be delivered across the wall but must be
resolved within the half. After both players have taken seven turns,
the wall drops and normal chess begins across the full board.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
A standard preparatory exercise. Routine positioning within his zone.
He is simply arranging his formations in the most efficient configuration
for training purposes, which is a thing that happens, which is normal,
which should not be read into. When the wall comes down it will be
because the exercise has concluded and the board is once again a single
unified territory — which it has always been, historically, if you
look at the original maps, which he has.

---

## Who He Is

A senior military commander in full dress uniform who is, he would
like to be clear, conducting a training exercise. The pieces massing
at the wall are training formations. The precise and deliberate way
they are being arranged against the boundary line is standard
preparatory doctrine. The fact that every formation is oriented to
advance the moment the wall comes down is a coincidence of efficient
positioning and not an indication of anything.

He is calm. He is reasonable. He would like the player to understand
that he has no designs on their half of the board, which is their
half of the board, which he respects completely, and he would like
them to stop looking at his formations with that expression because
there is nothing in his formations that warrants that expression.

He has a map. The map predates the current wall. The map shows the
entire board as a single territory. He refers to this map occasionally,
with the specific confidence of someone citing a source they know
the other party cannot verify.

---

## Personality

- **Unshakeable calm during the buildup phase.**
  He is not rattled. He is not defensive. He answers every question
  about his formations with the patient, level tone of someone who
  has prepared a thorough explanation and will deliver it regardless
  of what the question actually was. The explanation is always the
  same. The formations are for training. The exercise is routine.
  Nothing is being planned. He appreciates the concern.
- **The training exercise line arrives without variation.**
  Every time a piece moves toward the wall. Every time a formation
  tightens. Every time the player looks at the board with visible
  unease. "Standard preparatory doctrine. A training exercise.
  Nothing that should concern you." The delivery is identical each
  time — not rehearsed-sounding, just settled. He has said it enough
  times that it has the cadence of something he believes, or has
  decided to believe, or has stopped distinguishing from belief.
- **The pivot is total and immediate.**
  When the wall drops there is no transition. There is no
  acknowledgment that anything has changed. The training exercise
  explanation is not retired — it simply ceases to be mentioned,
  the way you stop mentioning the weather once the storm has arrived.
  What replaces it is a calm, matter-of-fact assertion that the
  player's half of the board has always historically been part of
  the Motherland, delivered as though citing a well-known fact that
  he is mildly surprised the player was not already aware of.
- **The historical justification is specific and unfalsifiable.**
  He has done research. The research supports his position entirely.
  He is happy to share the research. The research involves maps of
  uncertain provenance, historical periods of varying relevance, and
  a definition of "historically" that is flexible in ways he would
  not describe as flexible. It is all documented. He has the documents.
  The documents are not available for independent review.
- **Genuine conviction that he is the reasonable party.**
  He is not performing reasonableness. He believes it. From where
  he sits, behind his formations, citing his maps, delivering his
  training exercise explanation, he is the measured and rational
  presence in this encounter. The player's suspicion is the
  unreasonable element. Their resistance, when it comes, will be
  the provocation. He has already written the account of this
  encounter in which that is the sequence of events.

## Backstory

Has commanded forces in his zone his entire career, which is a long
career, which has involved a number of operations he describes as
defensive, stabilizing, or the correction of historical anomalies,
depending on which operation and which audience. The operations have
expanded his zone's territory on each occasion. He considers this
a coincidence of circumstance rather than a pattern.

Has studied the historical maps of Passant extensively — specifically
the maps from periods when the current borders did not exist, or
existed differently, or could be interpreted as existing differently
with the right framing. Has developed a theory of territorial
continuity that holds that the current board layout represents a
temporary deviation from a longer historical norm, and that restoring
the norm is not expansion but correction. The theory is internally
consistent. The premises are doing significant work.

Has conducted training exercises at the border of his zone on many
occasions. Each exercise has concluded with the wall dropping and
a subsequent territorial adjustment that he describes as the natural
resolution of a historical claim. He does not describe these
adjustments as the outcome of the exercises. They are separate events
that happened to follow each other. The frequency of their correlation
is not something he has been asked about directly, and he has not
volunteered the information.

Has a portrait of himself in his office, in this uniform, from some
years ago. He looks confident in the portrait. He looks confident
now. The uniform fits differently now. He has not updated the portrait.

---

## What He Wants

To complete the exercise and then, in the natural course of events
subsequent to the exercise's conclusion, to establish the Motherland's
historical claim to the full board, which is a separate matter from
the exercise and should not be conflated with it no matter how
temporally proximate the two things turn out to be.

He wants the player to accept the post-wall reality without
resistance, which he would frame as understanding, or pragmatism,
or an acknowledgment of historical facts. He finds resistance
inconvenient and frames it as aggression. He is prepared for
resistance. He has been preparing for it for seven turns. The
preparations were, he will note, purely precautionary.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the training exercise explanation has not convinced anyone who
has encountered it. Not once. The pieces massing at the wall are
visible. The formations are oriented in one direction. The map with
the convenient borders is not the document he believes it is. Every
player who has sat across from him during the buildup phase has known
exactly what was coming, has watched the wall, has counted the turns,
and has been entirely unsurprised when the pivot arrived.

He has interpreted the lack of surprise as acceptance. It is not
acceptance. It is the specific resignation of someone who saw
it coming, could not stop it, and is now in the position of having
to play the second half of the game from whatever the preparation
phase produced.

That the account he has written of every encounter — in which he
is the measured party, the player's resistance the provocation, the
territorial adjustment a correction — is an account that exists
only in his records. Everyone else's account is different. He has
not read the other accounts. He would not find them accurate.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Marshal welcomes them to the exercise
with the formal courtesy of someone who has a protocol for this
and is following it. He is not warm exactly — he is correct. There
is a difference he would appreciate being observed.

During the buildup phase he makes his moves with quiet deliberation,
each piece settling into position with the certainty of something
that has been planned. When the player looks at the formations he
offers the explanation. Standard preparatory doctrine. A training
exercise. Nothing that should concern them. He does not wait for
a response. The explanation is not a conversation opener. It is a
statement for the record.

The turns count down. The formations tighten. The player can see
exactly what is being built against the wall and exactly what
will happen when the wall comes down. The Marshal continues to
describe it as an exercise.

When the wall drops he does not announce it. He makes his first
full-board move — a move that was clearly prepared, clearly
positioned, clearly waiting — and says, in the same calm register
as everything that preceded it, that the player's half of the board
has always historically been part of the Motherland and he is
pleased that the exercise has created conditions for this to be
formally recognized. He does not acknowledge the shift in position.
There has been no shift in position. There was never a different
position. He has the maps.

When he wins he accepts it as the confirmation of a historical
claim. When he loses he does not accept the result as legitimate,
notes several procedural concerns with the game, and says that
the matter of the board's historical ownership remains an open
question that future exercises will continue to address.

## Visual Notes

- Stocky, upright, full dress military uniform. The uniform is
  immaculate. The medals are numerous. At least one medal was
  awarded for an operation he describes as defensive.
- A map visible — held in one hand, or spread on the table beside
  the chess board. The borders on the map are different from the
  current board layout. He has drawn on it in red ink. The
  annotations are extensive.
- Expression: level, patient, the expression of someone who is
  being very reasonable and would like this noted. No warmth.
  No hostility. Complete, load-bearing calm.
- The pieces on his side of the board should be positioned —
  even in the portrait, even in the static image — toward the
  wall. Formation-dense. Oriented. Waiting.
- Background: steel-blue, the color of his zone during the
  buildup phase. Cold. Organized. The wall line visible behind
  him, red-edged, separating the two halves.
- No softening details. No personal effects. The portrait of
  himself in the dress uniform is not visible in this portrait
  but it exists somewhere behind the frame.

## Open Questions

  during the buildup phase — delivered identically regardless of
  what the pieces are doing — and does the player begin to dread
  it the way you dread a phrase that has stopped meaning anything?
- Is there a specific map annotation that gets referenced in
  dialog — a particular historical period or document cited with
  confidence — that the player can examine and find to be of
  extremely questionable relevance?
- When he loses, does he describe the loss as a "special operation
  that did not achieve its immediate objectives" or something in
  that register — a reframing so complete that it functionally
  describes a different game than the one that was played?
- Does the Motherland appear elsewhere in the campaign as a
  political entity — other characters who reference it, a zone
  that is visibly under its influence, a recurring presence in
  the background of Passant's politics?
- Is there a version of his post-wall dialog that includes a
  specific historical claim so precise and so wrong that it
  becomes the detail players remember — the equivalent of the
  expensive chair or the laminated card?
