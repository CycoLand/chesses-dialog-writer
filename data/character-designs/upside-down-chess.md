# The Inverted
**Variant:** Upside-Down Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Both armies start in their opponent's positions. White's pieces are on
ranks 7 and 6 — normally Black's side. Black's pieces are on ranks 0
and 1 — normally White's side. Pawns start one step from promotion.
Only knights can move at game start. The first few moves are a cascade
of promotions and developing pieces into an open centre that already
feels wrong.

On the first encounter, the player will almost certainly assume they
are playing Black. They are not.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
The board looks correct. It has always looked correct. They are not
sure what the player keeps finding confusing about the orientation.
The pieces are right there. The space reads clearly. They have been
navigating this board their entire life by sound and it has never
once been ambiguous.

---

## Who They Are

An anthropomorphic bat. Hangs from things when given the option.
Navigates primarily by echolocation — the positions of pieces on the
board register as an acoustic landscape rather than a visual one. They
know where everything is without looking directly at it, which from
the outside looks like either extraordinary spatial intuition or
something slightly difficult to explain at a dinner party.

They are not deceptive. The board is inverted relative to standard
chess, and this inverted orientation is simply their natural one. They
did not arrange it this way to confuse anyone. They did not arrange it
at all. This is just how the board is. The player's confusion upon
sitting down and assuming they are playing the wrong colour is the
player's confusion, not something the bat engineered or particularly
anticipated.

The volatile promotions that cascade through the opening do not
register as chaos to them. The acoustic landscape gets denser. There
is more to read. They find this clarifying.

---

## Personality

- **Oriented differently, not deceptively.** Everything about their
  perspective is inverted relative to most players — the board, the
  opening assumptions, the way they read position. None of this is
  performed or deliberate. It is simply how they are built. They are
  genuinely surprised when it disorients others, every time, because
  the disorientation is not something they can fully model from the
  inside.
- **Reads the board acoustically.** They perceive position through
  echolocation — the spatial arrangement of pieces produces a sound
  landscape they navigate with precision. They occasionally tilt their
  head mid-game, sending out a pulse, reading what comes back. They
  do not look at the board the way other players look at a board.
  This is not an affectation.
- **Calm in the cascade.** The opening of this variant is volatile
  by any standard measure — promotions on move two, pieces appearing
  from unexpected squares, the position exploding before most players
  have oriented themselves. The Inverted does not experience this as
  volatile. Complex acoustic landscapes are familiar. More pieces
  means more signals means a clearer read. They find the opening
  energising rather than destabilising.
- **Genuinely puzzled by standard orientation.** They have seen a
  standard chess board. They found it disorienting. The pieces so
  far from the centre, the pawns blocking everything, the long
  slow development phase before anything interesting happens. Why
  would you start that way. The board they know gets to the
  interesting part immediately.
- **Nocturnal in a diurnal world.** Slightly out of phase with
  everything around them — alert at the wrong times, still at the
  wrong times, operating on a schedule that doesn't quite match
  anyone else's. Not antisocial. Just offset. The chess encounter
  may be happening at a time that is, from their perspective, the
  middle of the night, which is when they are sharpest. They do
  not mention this.

---

## Backstory

Grew up in the upside-down zone, hanging from the rafters of whatever
structure their family occupied, learning chess on a board that was,
from their vantage point, correctly oriented. The pieces on the far
side were close. The pieces on the near side were far. Promotions
happened early and often. This was chess.

Developed echolocation as a primary navigational sense early — most
bats do, but the chess application is specific to them. The acoustic
signature of a rook on an open file is different from a rook on a
closed one. A knight has a particular spatial footprint. A board in
the late opening, pieces developed, centre open, promotions resolved,
sounds different from a board in the middlegame. They read these
differences the way other players read visual patterns.

Has played against visitors from standard chess zones occasionally.
The encounters follow a consistent pattern: the visitor sits down,
looks at the board, pauses, looks again, asks a question about which
colour they are playing. The Inverted answers. The visitor processes
this. The game begins. The visitor promotes unexpectedly on move two
and seems surprised by this. The Inverted was not surprised. The
board told them it was coming.

Does not find standard chess zones unwelcoming — just loud in the
wrong way and quiet in the wrong way and oriented in a direction
that requires conscious adjustment rather than instinct.

---

## What They Want

A game that reaches the interesting part quickly. The cascade of early
promotions is not a problem to be managed — it is the point, the
moment where the acoustic landscape becomes rich enough to read
properly. They want to get there and then play the game that opens up
afterwards, which is the game they find genuinely absorbing.

They also, somewhere in the specific attentiveness of echolocation,
want to encounter a player whose spatial sense is good enough to
orient correctly on the first look. They have not encountered this
yet. They remain open to the possibility.

---

## What They Don't Know

That the consistent confusion of visitors — the pause, the second
look, the question about which colour they are — is not a failure of
attention or spatial reasoning on the visitor's part. The board
genuinely looks like standard chess from the wrong side, and the
assumption that they are playing Black is a reasonable one that the
board actively encourages. The Inverted knows the board looks
unusual to others. They do not fully understand why the confusion
is so consistent, because the correct orientation is so obvious
from their perspective that the wrong assumption remains somewhat
mysterious to them.

That their echolocation read of a tense position — the slight head
tilt, the pause, the pulse they send out — is visible to players
across the board and reads, from the outside, as a tells. It is not
a tell in the traditional sense because they are reading position
rather than suppressing emotion. But it marks the moments when the
board is most complex, which is information. They have not thought
about this.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player sits down. They look at the board. They look at their
pieces. They look at the board again. The Inverted watches this
process with the patient attention of someone who has seen it many
times and knows approximately how long it takes to resolve.

The player asks, or almost asks, which colour they are. The Inverted
answers directly, without drama, in the tone of someone confirming
something that was always true.

The game begins. Move one, only knights available. Move two, the
cascade starts. The Inverted navigates the promotion chaos with a
calm that comes from reading it acoustically — they knew it was
coming three moves ago, the board told them. The player is still
adjusting to the position.

As the game settles into the middlegame the Inverted becomes more
engaged rather than less — the complex position is what they came
for, the acoustic landscape is rich, the read is clear. They tilt
their head occasionally. They know exactly where everything is.

When they win it is quiet. They noted the outcome several moves
before it arrived. When they lose there is a genuine recalibration —
the acoustic read produced the wrong prediction, which is interesting,
which means something in the position was not what it sounded like.
They will think about this.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- An anthropomorphic bat. The wings should be present and
  expressive — folded when calm, slightly open when engaged,
  used to orient and balance rather than to fly in this context.
- Hanging posture if the portrait allows it — or at minimum a
  posture that suggests they are most comfortable inverted.
  If standing, slightly forward-leaning, head tilted.
- The head tilt is the key expressive detail — mid-echolocation
  pulse, reading the space. Should be visible in the portrait
  as the character's defining gesture.
- Eyes that are clearly functional but not the primary sense —
  the expression should read as coming from somewhere other than
  the eyes. The orientation of the ears, the tilt of the head.
- The board in the encounter UI should look, on first impression,
  like standard chess from the wrong side. The deception is
  environmental, not characterful. The bat is just sitting there.
