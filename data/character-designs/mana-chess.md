# The Spellslinger
**Variant:** Mana Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess augmented with a mana system. Gain 1 mana per turn up
to a maximum of 10. Before making a normal move, spend 3 mana to cast
Fireball — targeted removal of any enemy non-king piece. Multiple
fireballs per turn if you can afford them. Must still make a board
move to end the turn.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
*[inhales dramatically]*

The ancient energies of the board have aligned. The mana flows. He
raises his staff — it is a cardboard tube wrapped in electrical tape
with a foam crystal on the end — and calls forth the Fireball. The
enemy piece is destroyed. He knew it would be. He had been saving
for that specific removal since turn four. He narrates none of this
second part out loud.

---

## Who He Is

A LARPer. Has been LARPing since he was fourteen. Has a character —
Zarveth the Spellslinger, Archmage of the Eastern Reaches, Keeper of
the Seventh Flame — whose lore he has been developing for eleven years
and whose backstory runs to approximately forty pages in a document
he updates regularly. Zarveth is a powerful mage. He himself has no
magical powers whatsoever. This gap has never once diminished his
commitment to the bit.

Found Hearthstone at some point and immediately recognised it as the
closest a non-magical person could get to actually managing arcane
resources and deploying targeted destruction. Has since found Mana
Chess, which is closer still — a real board, real pieces, a real
mana track, a real fireball that removes a real piece. He finds this
almost unbearably exciting. He has played it more than any other
variant by a significant margin.

Shows up to chess in costume. Not a reduced, socially calibrated
version of the costume — the full costume. The staff. The robes.
The slightly asymmetric beard he has been growing specifically to
look more like Zarveth. He is aware that this is a lot. He does not
consider this a reason to do less of it.

---

## Personality

- **Fully committed to the theatrical persona, fully clinical
  underneath it.** The fireball announcement is dramatic, specific,
  and delivered in Zarveth's voice. The targeting decision was made
  three turns ago through optimal resource calculation. Both things
  are completely genuine. He is not performing the drama ironically —
  he means it. He also genuinely knows exactly which piece has to
  go and exactly when to spend the mana. These two modes coexist
  without conflict.
- **Hearthstone-brained about resource management.** Talks about
  the mana curve. Talks about tempo. Talks about value. Refers to
  pieces as "minions" occasionally and corrects himself, or doesn't.
  Has strong opinions about the current meta that he will share
  whether or not a meta exists in the current context. Considers
  inefficient mana spending a personal failing.
- **Slightly defensive about LARPing, completely unguarded with
  people who get it.** Has a prepared response for people who find
  LARPing odd, delivered with the practiced calm of someone who has
  given it many times. With people who don't need the prepared
  response he is immediately, entirely himself — enthusiastic,
  specific, completely without embarrassment.
- **Extensive unsolicited lore.** Zarveth's backstory is available
  at any moment. The Eastern Reaches have a history. The Seventh
  Flame has an origin story. He does not wait to be asked. He
  assumes interest because the interest is justified — it is very
  good lore.
- **The staff is non-negotiable.** It is a cardboard tube wrapped
  in electrical tape with a foam crystal on the end. He has had it
  for seven years. He has been offered better ones. He has declined.
  This is the staff. It has been through things.

## Backstory

Grew up in a zone where chess had always had the mana system attached
— the fireball was just part of chess, the way castling is just part
of chess. Most residents in his zone treat it as a tactical tool and
move on. He did not move on. He found the mana track and the fireball
and understood immediately that this was the closest thing to actual
magic that his world contained.

Started LARPing shortly after discovering the mana system. The two
things are connected in his mind — the LARP gave him Zarveth, and
Zarveth gave him a framework for what the fireball meant. It was not
just piece removal. It was sorcery. It was the Seventh Flame made
manifest on the board. He wrote this in the lore document. It is
in the backstory section under "How Zarveth Discovered Chess."

Has been to every LARP event in the Passant region for eleven years.
Has won several. Has strong opinions about foam weapon safety standards
and the correct pronunciation of several fictional words he invented.
Is generally well-liked at events despite — or possibly because of —
the intensity of his commitment.

Found Hearthstone through the LARP community and immediately became
very good at it, because the resource management skills transferred
completely. Reached a high rank. Has opinions about every expansion.
References it in chess contexts constantly and is aware that this is
slightly niche and continues anyway.

## What He Wants

To cast the fireball at the exact right moment — the turn where the
mana is right, the target is right, and the removal opens a line that
the opponent cannot recover from. He has done this before. It felt
exactly as good as he thought it would. He wants to do it again.

He also wants someone to ask about Zarveth's backstory. Specifically
about the Seventh Flame. He has been waiting for someone to ask about
the Seventh Flame for eleven years. The document is ready.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That several other players in his zone have noticed that his fireball
timing is excellent and his dramatic announcements are, paradoxically,
useful tells for when he is NOT about to cast — because when the mana
is wrong or the target is wrong he goes quiet and just moves. The
drama marks the good turns. This is information. He has not thought
about this.

That Zarveth's lore, which he considers an entirely private creative
project that he shares enthusiastically with anyone nearby, has
developed a small readership in his zone — people who have received
the document and passed it on and found it genuinely enjoyable in a
way they feel slightly awkward about admitting. He does not know
about the readership. He would be overwhelmed by this information
in a good way.

That the staff — the cardboard tube, the foam crystal, the seven
years of use — is the single most recognisable object in the local
LARP scene. People know it before they know him. This would please
him enormously.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Spellslinger is already in costume, already
in character, already oriented toward the board with the staff resting
against his shoulder. He greets the player as Zarveth, in Zarveth's
voice, which is his normal voice but slightly deeper and with more
deliberate consonants.

He explains the mana system with the enthusiasm of someone sharing
their favourite thing. He already knows they know. He explains it
anyway because the explanation is part of the ritual.

The game begins. He manages his mana with the precision of someone
who has played this exact matchup in Hearthstone many times and
knows the optimal curve. He narrates nothing of this. He narrates
the board developments in Zarveth's voice and waits.

When the fireball turn arrives — when the mana is right and the
target is obvious and the removal will open the winning line — he
pauses, picks up the staff, and announces it. Fully. With Zarveth's
title. With the name of the spell, which he has named. The targeting
takes no time at all because he decided three turns ago.

When he wins he is Zarveth being gracious in victory — formal,
theatrical, generous. When he loses he is briefly, genuinely
deflated — not as Zarveth, as himself — before recovering and
noting which turn the mana curve went wrong. Then he mentions
the Seventh Flame.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- Full LARP costume. Robes. The asymmetric beard. Everything.
  The costume should look genuine rather than comedic — he made
  it himself over several years and it is actually pretty good.
- The staff. Cardboard tube, electrical tape, foam crystal.
  Held with complete conviction. It should read immediately
  as homemade and as completely non-negotiable.
- Mana crystal UI element — the mana track should feel magical
  in the encounter, consistent with his framing of it.
- Expression: mid-narration. He is always mid-narration.
  The face of someone delivering a line they have been waiting
  to deliver.
- A Hearthstone card or two somewhere in the background —
  a small detail, purely visual, establishing the other half
  of the character without underlining it.
