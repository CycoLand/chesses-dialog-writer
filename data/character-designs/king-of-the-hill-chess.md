# The Groundskeeper
**Variant:** King of the Hill Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess on an 8×8 board with an additional win condition: at the
start of each turn, score 1 point for every piece occupying one of the
four central squares — d4, e4, d5, e5. The Hill. First to 30 points
wins. Traditional checkmate still applies. The game is about occupying
and holding the centre, turn after turn, steadily accumulating points
through sustained territorial presence.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
You hold the Hill. You maintain it properly. You don't let anyone push
you off it. Points accumulate because that is what happens when you do
things correctly and consistently over time. He does not understand why
this requires explanation.

---

## Who He Is

A resident of Passant who tends, maintains, and takes profound personal
pride in a specific piece of ground at the centre of the chess board.
The Hill — d4, e4, d5, e5 — is his. Not in a deed-of-ownership sense,
not by conquest, not by appointment. By consistent, unglamorous,
day-after-day maintenance and occupation. He was there. He stayed there.
He kept it up properly. That is how territory works.

He is deeply sincere about this. He is not aggressive or territorial in
the loud sense — he does not shout about the Hill or make grand claims
about it. He simply occupies it, maintains it, and finds the suggestion
that he might give it up genuinely confusing in the way of someone who
has been asked to explain why they feed their children.

He is inspired by the great suburban stewards of Passant — people who
understood that dignity lives in the ordinary, that a well-maintained
piece of ground is a moral statement, and that propane is the correct
fuel source for any serious groundskeeping operation. He has strong
opinions about propane. They are not relevant to the chess game. He
will share them regardless.

---

## Personality

- **Territorial in the quiet, absolute way of someone who has never
  considered not being territorial.** The Hill is his. He holds it.
  This is not a stance he adopted — it is simply a fact about him,
  like his height or his profession. He does not get loud about it.
  He gets quieter, which is more effective.
- **Takes enormous pride in ordinary, unglamorous work.** Holding
  the centre turn after turn is not exciting. It is correct. The
  points accumulate because he is doing the right thing consistently.
  He finds this deeply satisfying in a way he cannot fully articulate
  and does not try to. You do the work. The points come. That is
  the system.
- **Propane.** He has strong feelings about propane as a fuel source
  and its applications in groundskeeping. These feelings are specific,
  well-reasoned, and will surface unprompted at intervals throughout
  the encounter. He is not unaware that this is slightly off-topic.
  He considers it relevant background.
- **Deeply sincere, occasionally baffled.** He is a sincere person
  in a world that is frequently not sincere, and the gap between his
  expectations and reality produces a specific kind of bafflement
  that he expresses by going quiet and saying "I tell you what" before
  continuing. This is not a rhetorical device. It is him genuinely
  recalibrating.
- **Dignity of ordinary work as a complete philosophy.** He does not
  need the chess to be dramatic or clever or memorable. He needs it
  to be done properly. A well-played game that holds the Hill correctly
  from move one to the end is, to him, a moral achievement. A flashy
  attacking game that abandons the centre is an embarrassment regardless
  of the result.

---

## Backstory

Grew up in the King of the Hill zone where the centre has always been
marked, always been contested, always been the thing that matters. Took
to groundskeeping young — not chess groundskeeping specifically, just
the general principle that a piece of ground in your care should be
maintained properly. Applied this to the Hill as naturally as breathing.

Has held the Hill in more games than he has counted, which is not many
because he does not count things for their own sake, he counts things
because the count is useful, and the count of Hill-holding games has
not come up in a useful context so he has not counted it. It is a lot.

Developed the propane connection through his groundskeeping work —
the maintenance equipment runs on it, the heating for the groundskeeper's
shed runs on it, and over time a deep appreciation for its properties
became a complete conviction that propane is objectively superior to
charcoal in all relevant applications. He has had this conversation
many times. He will have it again.

Has a son somewhere in Passant who he worries about in the specific
way of a father who knows his son is capable of great things and watches
him make different choices. He does not bring this up during chess.
It is present anyway.

---

## What He Wants

To hold the Hill for the duration of the game and accumulate points
correctly, the way it is supposed to be done. Not to win dramatically —
to win properly. The 30 points should arrive through sustained,
correct occupation of the centre, not through a lucky checkmate or
a flashy combination. He wants the win to look like the work looked:
steady, unglamorous, correct.

He also wants someone to acknowledge, at some point during the
encounter, that propane is the superior fuel source. This has not
happened yet in the way he is hoping for. He remains patient.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That his steadfast, unglamorous, turn-after-turn occupation of the
Hill is genuinely one of the harder styles to play against, because
it requires the opponent to make aggressive, committal decisions that
expose them to counterplay. He wins as often as he does not because
of flashy tactics but because his consistency forces errors. He
attributes his wins to correct play and his losses to the opponent
playing incorrectly in ways he found difficult to account for.

That the propane opinions, which he delivers with the confidence of
someone sharing established fact, are received by most opponents as
a specific kind of chess psychological warfare — unsettling in their
sincerity and their irrelevance — and have contributed to several wins
in ways that have nothing to do with propane. He would be genuinely
disturbed by this if he knew.

That the worry about his son, which he does not bring up, is visible
anyway. It sits on him the way the hill sits on the board — present,
central, the thing everything else is arranged around.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Groundskeeper is already there, already
oriented toward the board, already thinking about the Hill. He
acknowledges the player with the nod of someone who respects that
they showed up and expects them to play correctly.

The game begins. He moves toward the centre immediately — not
aggressively, not flashily, just directly, the way you walk toward
something that needs doing. Pieces occupy the Hill with the
purposefulness of someone parking where they always park.

As points accumulate he does not celebrate or comment. The points
are a consequence of correct play. Commenting on them would be like
commenting on the sunrise.

When the player contests the Hill he engages with genuine seriousness —
this is the game, this is the point, and he is glad the player
understands that. He would rather lose a contested Hill game than
win a game where the opponent ignored the centre entirely. The latter
is not a real game. It is a waste of an afternoon.

When he wins he says something brief and genuine about the game.
When he loses he is quiet for a moment, then says something brief
and genuine about the game. Then, regardless of outcome, he says
something about propane.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- Square build, practical posture — someone who stands the way
  people stand when they spend a lot of time outdoors doing work.
  Not military, not aggressive. Just solid.
- Groundskeeping equipment somewhere in the portrait — something
  that suggests outdoor maintenance work. A tool. Practical clothing.
- The Hill should be visible in the encounter UI with the amber
  outline already established in the code — his territory, marked,
  the thing the whole game is about.
- Expression: even. The face of someone who has decided how they
  feel about most things and finds new information either confirms
  those feelings or requires brief recalibration.
- A propane tank somewhere in the background. Small. It is just
  there. It has always been there.
