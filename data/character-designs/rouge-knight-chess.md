# The Knight Trainer
**Variant:** Rouge Knight Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess with one addition: a neutral crimson knight — belonging
to neither player — starts on a random center square at the beginning
of the game. After each player's turn, the rouge knight moves
autonomously, always targeting the highest-value piece it can reach,
otherwise moving toward the center. Neither player can capture it. It
can put kings in check. Both players must work around something neither
of them controls and neither of them can remove.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Biscuit is just playing. He gets a little excited with new people —
that's completely normal, that's actually a sign of engagement, which
is a positive training indicator — and he'll settle down once he's had
a chance to burn off some energy. She would appreciate it if the player
didn't make any sudden movements. Biscuit responds to sudden movements.
She's working on that.

---

## Who She Is

A professional knight trainer with several years of experience, a
certification she will mention, and a crimson knight named Biscuit who
has been loose on the board since the start of the game and has so far
taken a pawn, a bishop, and is currently positioning to threaten
something significantly more valuable.

She is not embarrassed. She would like to be clear that she is not
embarrassed. This is part of the process. Training is not linear.
Biscuit is in what she would describe, professionally, as an
exploratory phase, which is a phase she has been describing him as
being in for longer than she has been precisely tracking. The training
is going well. She has a clicker.

The clicker has not worked today. It has also not worked on any of
the previous days she has used it, though she does not think of it
this way. She thinks of it as: the clicker works, and today Biscuit
is distracted, and when he is less distracted the clicker will work,
and the fact that he is always distracted is a separate issue she is
also working on.

She loves Biscuit. This is the most important fact about her and
also the explanation for everything else.

---

## Personality

- **Unconditional positive regard, applied without limit.**
  There is no behavior Biscuit can produce that she will not find
  a reframe for. He took a rook? He's showing initiative. He put
  the opponent's king in check? He's very perceptive, actually,
  he has excellent spatial awareness, this is something she's been
  nurturing. He is currently on the wrong side of the board eating
  a knight that was not doing anything to him? He's curious. You
  want a curious knight. Curiosity is trainable. Apathy isn't.
- **The professional credentials arrive early and often.**
  She is certified. The certification is from an institution she
  names with confidence. She has completed the advanced module. She
  has references. The references are from before Biscuit. She does
  not consider this relevant because the principles are universal
  and Biscuit is a special case which she is managing with the
  universal principles.
- **The clicker.**
  She clicks it before several of her turns. She clicks it when
  Biscuit moves. She clicks it when Biscuit takes a piece, at
  a slightly different rhythm, which she would explain is a
  corrective signal if asked. She has not been asked. The clicking
  continues. Biscuit does not respond to the clicking. She clicks
  it again, with slightly more confidence, as if the previous
  click was a warmup.
- **Explanations, not apologies.**
  She does not apologize for Biscuit. She explains him. The
  explanation is always available, always specific, and always
  concludes that what Biscuit did was either fine or a sign of
  progress. She has an extensive vocabulary for reframing. She
  has been developing this vocabulary for as long as she has
  had Biscuit.
- **The treats.**
  There are treats in her left pocket. They are Biscuit's
  favorite — she made them herself, a specific recipe she
  developed during what she calls the "recall work phase,"
  which has been ongoing. She has held one out twice during
  the game so far. Biscuit has not come. She has put it
  back and taken out a different one. This one is also not
  working. She has three more in the pocket. She is confident
  about the third one.

## Backstory

Came to knight training through a genuine love of the pieces —
the movement, the unpredictability, the way a knight sees the board
differently from every other piece. Got her certification. Took on
clients. Had a solid early career working with standard knights who
responded to standard methods and produced standard results and
went home at the end of the day to their armies where they belonged.

Then she got Biscuit.

Biscuit was described to her as "a challenge." She heard this as
an opportunity. She was not wrong that it was an opportunity —
she has learned more from Biscuit than from any other subject in
her career — but the opportunity has not resolved in the direction
she expected. Biscuit does not belong to an army. Biscuit does not
respond to commands. Biscuit responds to nothing, technically, and
yet she has not described him this way because describing him this
way would require a conclusion she is not prepared to reach.

Has played chess in the zone her whole life and has developed, without
fully noticing, a genuine expertise in working around Biscuit's
autonomous movement. She reads his greedy policy the way you read
the weather — not to control it, but to anticipate it and plan
accordingly. She has never described this as a skill because
describing it as a skill would mean acknowledging that controlling
Biscuit is not the skill she has developed. She is still working
on the controlling. The anticipating has gotten very good.

---

## What She Wants

To finish this chess game and also, before it ends, to get Biscuit
to respond to at least one recall command in front of a witness,
which would be progress she could document. She has a notebook.
She has been documenting. The documentation shows a training
timeline that she describes as non-linear and others would describe
differently.

She wants the player to understand that what they are seeing is not
representative of Biscuit's full capabilities, which are significant,
and that the current behavior is a phase, and that she appreciates
their patience, and that Biscuit is a good boy.

---

## What She Doesn't Know

That every player who has come through her zone has had the same
experience: a chess game interrupted repeatedly by Biscuit, a
trainer clicking a clicker with undiminished confidence, and a
specific feeling afterward of having watched something that was
both a chess game and not really a chess game, and not being
sure which part was the point.

That the expertise she has genuinely developed — reading Biscuit's
movement, planning around his autonomous greed, treating his
presence as a permanent feature of the landscape rather than a
problem to be solved — is real and hard-won and would make her
an extraordinary teacher if she ever acknowledged it was what
she was teaching.

That the clicker has never worked. Not once. She has clicked it
four hundred and twelve times across all games played in this zone.
The number is in the notebook. She has not added up the column.

That Biscuit is, by every observable metric, having a wonderful
time. She knows this. She calls it a positive indicator. She does
not know that she is right.

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Knight Trainer welcomes them warmly and
immediately begins managing their expectations about Biscuit in
a way that raises more concerns than it addresses. Biscuit is on
the board. She gestures at him with the affection of someone
introducing a family member they are also slightly monitoring.

The chess begins. It is, structurally, a normal chess game
interrupted at intervals by Biscuit. She plays thoughtfully and
with genuine skill — her positional instincts are good, shaped
by years of planning around an unpredictable autonomous actor —
while also tracking Biscuit in her peripheral vision and clicking
the clicker at moments that suggest a methodology without quite
producing one.

When Biscuit takes one of the player's pieces she produces an
explanation immediately. The explanation is specific. It is about
Biscuit. It does not include an apology. She then holds out a
treat in Biscuit's direction while making her next chess move with
her other hand. Biscuit does not come. She puts the treat back.
The chess move was good.

When Biscuit takes one of her own pieces she is quiet for a moment.
Then she nods slowly, in the manner of someone incorporating new
data into an existing theory. "That's interesting," she says. She
clicks the clicker once. She moves her piece.

When she wins she is warm and genuine and thanks the player for
their patience with Biscuit, who she notes was much better today
than last time. When she loses she also thanks the player for their
patience with Biscuit, and notes that the training is ongoing, and
asks if they noticed that Biscuit responded to the clicker on move
fourteen, which he did not, and she is not certain she believes
it herself, and she clicks the clicker once more on the way out.

## Visual Notes

- Human woman, practical clothing — the clothing of someone who
  spends time with animals and has dressed accordingly. Sensible
  shoes. A pocket on the left side of her jacket that has seen
  a lot of use.
- The clicker visible in one hand. Held with the ease of something
  carried so constantly it no longer registers as being carried.
- Expression: the patient, attentive look of someone monitoring
  a situation they are confident they have under control. The
  confidence is genuine. The control is not present.
- Biscuit visible in the portrait — the crimson knight somewhere
  in the background, mid-movement, clearly going somewhere she
  did not direct him. She is not looking at him in the portrait.
  She is looking at the player. This is either trust or denial
  and possibly both.
- Background: her training zone — a chess board environment with
  the center squares visible, one of them recently vacated by
  Biscuit. The treats in the left pocket should be suggested
  rather than shown.
- A small notebook or training log visible somewhere. The column
  of clicker attempts is in it. She has not added it up.

## Open Questions

- Does Biscuit have a specific behavior trigger — a line she
  delivers every time he takes a piece, varying slightly each
  time, always an explanation never an apology — that the player
  begins to anticipate by the third or fourth instance?
- Is there a moment where Biscuit takes one of her most valuable
  pieces and she is silent for longer than usual before producing
  the explanation? What does the silence sound like?
- Does the notebook appear as an item in the campaign — something
  the player can examine — and if so what does the training log
  actually say? Does the data, read honestly, tell a story she
  hasn't read in it?
- Is there a version of the recall command that almost works —
  a moment where Biscuit pauses, seemingly responding, before
  moving away anyway — and does she catch it and document it
  with a specific, slightly heartbreaking optimism?
- Does Biscuit appear elsewhere in the campaign, loose, in zones
  he has no business being in, with no trainer in sight?
