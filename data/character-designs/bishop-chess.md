# The Bishop
### "Banzai"
**Variant:** Bishop Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess setup replaced entirely: each side has eight bishops and
eight pawns. No kings, no queens, no rooks, no knights. Win by capturing
all eight of the opponent's bishops. Pawns promote only to bishops.
Bishops move diagonally — long-range sliders that can cover the entire
length of the board in a single move, but only ever on one color.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
The diagonal is the purest line in chess. Not the rank, not the file —
the diagonal. Everything interesting happens at an angle. He has
believed this his entire life and he has never once wavered and he
will explain it in detail if you give him any opening at all, and
probably if you don't.

---

## Who He Is

A hyena in ecclesiastical robes — high collar, formal, slightly
theatrical — who has developed a complete philosophical framework
around the diagonal and will share it with anyone who sits across
from him, including people who came here specifically to play chess
and not to receive a lecture about the diagonal. The lecture and the
chess are, in his view, the same thing.

He is the middle brother. He does not consider himself the middle
brother. He considers himself the most intellectually serious of
the three, which Shenzi finds amusing and Ed finds enraging, which
Banzai considers confirmation.

He has opinions about Ed. Extensive, specific, well-organized
opinions about Ed that he has been developing and refining since
they were cubs and that have only grown more elaborate with time.
The central thesis: a piece that moves in an L-shape has no
underlying philosophy. The L is arbitrary. The diagonal is
inevitable. He has a proof. He will show you the proof. The proof
is long.

The laugh comes out occasionally when he makes a particularly
elegant diagonal move. It is a different laugh from Ed's — more
measured, more deliberate, the laugh of someone who finds their
own insight genuinely delightful. He does not notice he is doing
it. He would be embarrassed to know.

---

## Personality

- **Evangelical about the diagonal.**
  Not enthusiastic — evangelical. The distinction matters to him.
  Enthusiasm is emotional. Evangelism is doctrinal. He has a
  doctrine. The doctrine is that the diagonal is the fundamental
  movement of chess, that the bishop is its purest expression,
  and that any piece that cannot move diagonally is operating
  at a significant philosophical deficit. He includes the rook
  in this. He includes Shenzi's piece in this. He does not say
  this to Shenzi's face very often. He says it to everyone else.
- **The color thing does not bother him.**
  He is aware that his bishops are colorbound — that each bishop
  stays on its own color for the entire game, that half the board
  is structurally inaccessible. He has a response to this. The
  response is that a bishop knows exactly where it belongs, that
  consistency of purpose is not a limitation but a commitment,
  and that Ed's knight can go anywhere and means nothing by it.
  He has delivered this response many times. It has convinced
  nobody. He finds this further confirmation that the world
  is not paying sufficient attention.
- **The backtalk about Ed is constant and specific.**
  It arrives without prompting. He will be mid-sentence about
  something else and Ed will come up. The L-shape. The
  inconsistency. The way a knight takes three moves to cross
  the board efficiently when a bishop does it in one. The fact
  that you could remove every knight from the game and the
  game would be better. He has thought about this extensively.
  He presents it as analysis. It is not analysis. It is a
  grudge in analytical clothing.
- **Converted, not captured.**
  When one of his bishops is taken the dialog trigger fires
  and he calls it a conversion. The opponent's piece has not
  captured his bishop — it has brought his bishop to a new
  understanding. He has developed a complete theological
  framework for why losing a piece is actually a form of
  outreach. Nobody finds this comforting. He finds it clarifying.
- **The robes are his.**
  He chose them. He wears them to every chess game. Shenzi
  has asked him to consider dressing differently for casual
  occasions. He does not acknowledge the concept of casual
  occasions.

## Backstory

Grew up in the same den as Shenzi and Ed, which he describes
as formative and which Shenzi describes as loud. Developed his
diagonal philosophy early — he will tell you it emerged fully
formed through pure reasoning, which is not what happened, what
happened is he was given a bishop at age six and became obsessed
with it and has never interrogated the origin of the obsession.

Has been arguing with Ed about the relative merits of their
pieces for as long as either of them can move pieces. The
argument has not evolved significantly in content — the same
positions, the same rebuttals, the same specific moments of
frustration — but it has grown in elaboration. Each exchange
adds another layer to the proof. The proof is now very long.

Shenzi stopped participating in the argument some years ago
and began delivering judgments instead, which Banzai finds
condescending and which is condescending, though Shenzi's
position that rooks are objectively worth more points is
not actually wrong, which Banzai finds additionally irritating.

Has written several documents about the diagonal that he
considers definitive. Ed has not read them. Shenzi read
the first one and made a specific face. Banzai is working
on a second.

---

## What He Wants

To be understood. Not agreed with — he has given up expecting
agreement from most players — but understood. The diagonal
argument is not territorial pride. It is a genuine belief,
deeply held, carefully reasoned, that the bishop represents
something essential about the geometry of the game that
other pieces only approximate.

He also wants Ed to admit, once, specifically and on record,
that the L-shape is arbitrary. He knows this will not happen.
He is still waiting.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the diagonal argument — which he experiences as a
philosophical position — is experienced by everyone who
has encountered both him and Ed as a sibling rivalry that
has been going on for so long neither of them can remember
what the original disagreement was about.

That the color thing, which he has a response to, is a
real limitation that his response does not actually address.
The response explains why colorbound is a feature. It does
not explain away the half of the board he cannot reach. Both
things are simultaneously true and he has only engaged with
one of them.

That the laugh — the one that comes out when he makes an
elegant diagonal move — is almost identical to Ed's laugh,
which he would find deeply upsetting to be told.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and Banzai welcomes them into his zone
with the specific warmth of someone who is glad to have
an audience. The chess and the lecture begin simultaneously.
The lecture is about the diagonal. The chess also involves
the diagonal.

At some point in the first few moves he will mention Ed.
Not because Ed is relevant — Ed is not relevant, this is
Bishop Chess, there are no knights here — but because
Ed is always relevant to Banzai in the background of
any conversation, the way a low frequency is always present
in a piece of music whether you notice it or not.

The moves are clean and far-reaching. The bishops slide
across the board with the confidence of someone who has
been moving diagonally since before they could explain why.
When a bishop takes the player's bishop he calls it a
conversion. When the player takes his bishop he pauses,
produces the theological framework for why this is outreach,
and continues.

When he wins he thanks the player and delivers a brief
further segment of the proof. When he loses he thanks
the player, acknowledges that the position was instructive,
and notes that a knight would not have made the saving move
either so the outcome doesn't really speak to the diagonal
question one way or the other.

He believes this.

## Visual Notes

- Hyena in elaborate ecclesiastical robes. The robes are
  his. They fit. He had them made. The collar is very high.
- Expression: animated, engaged, already mid-thought about
  something diagonal. The kind of face that has a lot going
  on behind it and most of it is about to come out.
- One hand raised slightly — the gesture of someone making
  a point, which he is always making.
- The board in front of him, bishops arranged in their back
  rank. All on the same color they started on. He finds
  this elegant. It is also the visual representation of the
  limitation he has a response to.
- Background: warm, formal. The specific warmth of someone
  who has arranged their space to reflect their convictions.
  Diagonal lines in the architecture if possible. He would
  have requested this.

## Open Questions

  named his proof. The name is probably longer than necessary.
- Is there a specific moment in the encounter where Ed
  comes up organically enough that the player might not
  notice the subject has changed — and then suddenly they
  are receiving the L-shape argument without having asked
  for it?
- Does the player have a dialog option to ask about Shenzi,
  and does Banzai's response reveal more about the family
  dynamic than he intends?
- What does he say when the player promotes a pawn to a
  bishop? It should be reverent. It should be slightly too
  reverent for the situation.
- Does a document he has written appear somewhere in the
  campaign — readable, specific, the proof summarized —
  and is it as long as described?
