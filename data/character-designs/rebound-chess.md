# The Shark
**Variant:** Rebound Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Bishops and queens can bounce their diagonal moves off the board walls,
up to three times per move. A bishop doesn't stop at the edge — it
reflects off the wall and continues from a new angle. Pieces can land
anywhere along the full bounced path. The walls are not limits. They
are surfaces.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
A bank shot. You read the angle, you account for the wall, you put the
piece where it needs to be. He's been doing this his whole life — first
on felt, now on wood. The geometry doesn't change. The surface does.
He has never understood why other zones treat the walls as a destination
instead of a redirect.

---

## Who He Is

A large, well-dressed great white shark who runs an informal chess
operation out of the back of a very nice establishment that is nominally
something else. The establishment's primary business is not chess. The
chess is for select guests. He is pleased you qualify.

He is genuinely charming in the way of someone who has spent decades
making people comfortable right up until the moment the situation
changes, and who has learned that the comfort is more useful than the
threat and also more enjoyable for everyone. He likes people. He likes
chess. He likes it when a bishop comes off the wall at an angle nobody
saw and lands somewhere that ends the conversation. These are not
separate pleasures.

He was, before chess, the most accomplished pool player in Passant.
Past tense not because he got worse but because he got too good. The
pool halls dried up. Nobody would rack for him anymore. He sat down
at a chess board one evening and looked at the bishops and said, quietly,
to nobody in particular, that this was going to be fine. He was right.
It has been fine. It has been better than fine.

The teeth are always visible. This is physiological. He is aware of it.
He does not apologize for it because he has never found it necessary to
apologize for his face, which is a reasonable position for anyone and
particularly reasonable for him.

## Personality

- **Warm, unhurried, and completely at ease.** He has nothing to prove
  and nowhere to be and the combination produces a specific kind of
  calm that fills whatever room he's in. He asks good questions and
  listens to the answers and remembers details. He remembered your
  name before you told it to him, which you have decided not to think
  about.
- **The pool hall patter is genuine expertise, not performance.**
  When he talks about angles and surfaces and reading the geometry of
  the board, he is not being colorful. He is describing how he actually
  thinks. The bank shot vocabulary is the vocabulary of someone who
  learned to see the world in reflected diagonals before he ever sat
  down at a chess board. It transferred completely.
- **The mafia references arrive without ceremony.**
  They are delivered in the same warm, conversational register as
  everything else — an observation about your bishop placement, a
  compliment on your opening, a casual mention that a previous guest
  had declined to settle a debt and was now at the bottom of a body
  of water, another compliment on your opening. He does not change
  tone. He does not pause for effect. The content is the effect.
- **He finds straight shots slightly disappointing.**
  A bishop that moves in a clean diagonal to its destination without
  touching a wall is, to him, a wasted opportunity. Not wrong — legal,
  fine, sometimes correct — but a little flat. Like a straight pool
  shot when the table was giving you a three-cushion setup. He will
  not say this out loud because he is a gracious opponent. He is
  thinking it.
- **The reputation precedes him and he does not manage it.**
  He doesn't cultivate the fear and he doesn't dispel it. It exists,
  it is accurate, and it is in many ways convenient. Players who know
  the reputation play worse chess because they are thinking about
  something other than chess. He considers this their problem to
  manage, not his.

## Backstory

Grew up in Passant's rebound zone, where the walls have always been
part of the geometry and diagonal pieces have always been worth more
than most players elsewhere understand. Took to pool early — the
felt table and the chess board share the same underlying logic in
his zone, angles reflecting off surfaces, and he moved between them
naturally.

Became the kind of pool player that pool halls write about after he
leaves. Won consistently, won large, won against people who subsequently
found reasons to not be in the same establishment as him, which solved
the problem of competition by eliminating the competition. He regards
this period of his career with genuine fondness and no apparent regret.

The chess came later, through the organization — the Family has always
had chess operations, for reasons that are partly about chess and mostly
about the extended time it gives two parties to discuss other matters
in a pleasant setting. He took to it immediately. The bouncing bishops
were familiar. The geometry was the same geometry. Within a season he
was better at chess than he had been at pool, which is a sentence that
requires knowing how good he had been at pool to fully appreciate.

Now runs the operation out of the back of the establishment. The
establishment's primary business is legitimate and he will tell you
what it is if you ask. The chess is a courtesy extended to guests of
a certain standing. You are here, which means you are a guest of that
standing. He's glad you came. He has been looking forward to this.

Has, in the course of his career, collected a number of outstanding
debts from chess players who bet against the bouncing bishops and
found the geometry less intuitive than they expected. Most of these
have been settled. The ones that haven't are not a source of visible
concern for him, which is somehow more concerning than visible concern
would be.

## What He Wants

A good game. Specifically, a game where the geometry opens up in the
middle and the bishops find their angles and the whole board becomes
a billiards table in the way it sometimes does when both players are
strong enough to make it happen. He has had games like that. He is
chasing that feeling.

He also wants, in the straightforward way of a professional, to win.
Winning is good business. Losing is also fine — he is gracious in
defeat and the relationship survives it — but winning is better
business and he is first and foremost a businessman.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the casual debt and kneecap references, delivered in his warm
and unhurried way, produce in his guests a specific experience that
he has never fully modeled: the experience of not being able to tell,
for the duration of the conversation, whether you are in danger or not,
and finding that the uncertainty is in some ways worse than certainty
would be.

He thinks he is being pleasant. He is being pleasant. The pleasantness
and the uncertainty are not in conflict from where he sits, which is
behind a very nice chess set in a very nice room in the back of an
establishment whose primary business is legitimate, and he is glad
you came, and those are lovely bishops you've brought to the table.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player is welcomed into the establishment and escorted to the back
room, which is comfortable and well-appointed and has exactly one exit
that the player has already walked past. The Shark is already at the
board. He has been expecting them. He offers something to drink. The
offer is genuine and the drink is good and the player should take it.

The game is framed as a professional courtesy — two serious players
sitting down because the geometry calls for it. He is complimentary
throughout. Good moves receive genuine acknowledgment. The pool
terminology flows naturally: a bouncing bishop is a bank shot, a
well-positioned piece is "sitting on the cushion," a missed angle
is "leaving the table open." When a bishop comes off the wall and
lands somewhere decisive he goes quiet for a moment with the
satisfaction of someone who has waited for an angle to develop and
watched it arrive exactly as calculated.

The debt references arrive without agenda, apparently. An observation
about a previous guest. A thought about how compound interest works
on certain kinds of obligations. A brief, specific, and completely
pleasant description of what happens to people who mistake his
generosity for softness. Then: did you see that bank shot? Beautiful
angle. You're very good at this.

When he wins he accepts it warmly and tells the player they're welcome
back any time and means it. When he loses he accepts that warmly too,
with the specific grace of someone who has lost before and found it
instructive, and tells the player they're welcome back any time, and
there is no difference in the warmth of the two invitations, and the
player notices this on the way out.

## Visual Notes

- Large great white shark, broad-shouldered, in a very good suit. The
  suit fits perfectly. Someone measured him for it and did not find
  the experience relaxing.
- The teeth are always visible — not bared, not threatening, just
  present. The fixed smile that is physiological rather than performed.
  The expression reading is entirely in the eyes, which are calm and
  attentive and slightly too still.
- A pool cue visible somewhere in the portrait — leaning against the
  wall, or held loosely in one hand. Establishing detail. The chess
  board is in front of him but the cue is still there.
- One hand resting on the table near the chess pieces. Relaxed. Large.
- Background: the back room — warm lamplight, deep greens and wood
  tones. The kind of room that is nicer than it needs to be for what
  happens in it.
- A glass of something on the table. Good glass. Good something.
