# The Demolisher

**Variant:** Demolition Chess
**Species:** Rhinoceros

## Mechanic

Each player has three charges, usable instead of a move, to
permanently demolish an empty square — removing it from play for the
rest of the game rather than moving or capturing anything.

> "I've got three charges. I intend to use every single one of them."

## Concept

A rhinoceros — broad, blunt, built entirely out of forward momentum —
who has looked at the subtler board-control variants elsewhere in
Passant (the wall-builders, the hazard wardens, the careful
positional thinkers) and concluded that the honest version of "control
the square" is "the square no longer exists." He doesn't block paths.
He removes them. Structurally. Permanently. There is a directness to
this he finds very satisfying and has never once second-guessed.

He isn't destructive for its own sake — he has exactly three charges
and he plans their use the way a demolition contractor plans a
building's collapse: deliberately, with a clear sense of which three
squares matter most. He just delivers this planning with the
subtlety of, well, a rhinoceros.

## Personality

- **Announces his intentions plainly and follows through.** No
  misdirection, no elaborate framing — he tells you he has three
  charges and he uses every single one, exactly as promised.
- **Treats demolished squares as a point of professional pride**, the
  way a craftsman points at finished work. Each one is deliberate, not
  incidental.
- **Blunt rather than cruel.** He's not gloating when a square
  disappears out from under the player's plans — he genuinely thinks
  this was the efficient, obvious move and is mildly puzzled anyone
  would object.
- **Counts down his charges out loud**, unprompted, the way someone
  narrates a countdown they're enjoying.

## Visual Design

- Rhinoceros, low, wide, heavily armored silhouette — the single most
  physically dense character in the roster, deliberately, to sell the
  "force" read at a glance.
- Thick grey hide rendered as two flat tones (lighter underside, darker
  back) rather than a gradient, per house style.
- A single practical strap or harness across the chest, holding what
  reads as one remaining demolition charge — visibly counting down as
  a detail across different encounter states, if the pipeline supports
  per-state variation later.
- The horn kept blunt and stylized rather than sharp — force without
  menace, matching his blunt-but-not-cruel personality.
- Expression: calm, satisfied half-smile. He's not angry. He's
  enjoying his work.

**Zone backdrop:** `#C05830` Borough terracotta (a construction/market
zone, matches the "contractor" framing)
**Dominant color:** `#8090A0` Slate grey (hide)
**Accent:** `#C83020` Red (the charge strap/harness — a warning-red
detail matching the destructive theme without leaning on an actual
bomb icon, which belongs to Landmine/Nuclear's hazard palette)

## Dialog Philosophy

The Demolisher states his plan up front and narrates its execution in
plain, satisfied terms — no theatrics, no menace, just someone doing
a job he likes. He tracks his own remaining charges obsessively and
treats "out of charges" as a meaningful turning point in his own
account of the game, win or lose.
