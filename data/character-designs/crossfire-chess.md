# The Good Boy
**Variant:** Crossfire Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
An L-shaped 10×10 board. White starts at the bottom advancing north
as normal. Black starts on the right side of the board advancing west.
Two armies on perpendicular trajectories converging toward the center.
The opponent's pieces are not coming at you — they are coming across
you, from a direction that standard chess provides no preparation for.
The crossfire zone is where the two paths inevitably meet.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Is this right? Is this where he's supposed to be? He went this way
because it seemed like the way to go and now here he is and there's
something over there that might be a piece or might be a threat and
should he — is that — wait, is that a capture? It was a capture.
Was that good?

---

## Who He Is

A medium-sized dog of indeterminate breed who plays chess in his zone
because chess is what is happening here and he would like to be part
of what is happening. He is not certain he understands chess. He is
not certain he doesn't. He has been told he is quite good and he has
also been told he needs to focus and both of these have been said to
him by the same people sometimes in the same sentence and he finds
this confusing, which is fine, because he finds most things confusing,
which has never once stopped him from engaging with them fully and
immediately.

He approaches everything from a slight angle. Not metaphorically —
physically. His head is tilted. It is always tilted. There is
something about the world that he is perpetually on the verge of
working out and the working out of it requires the head to be at
approximately seventeen degrees from vertical and he has held this
angle for so long that it is simply how he is now. Nobody has
mentioned it to him. He would not know what to do with the information.

He is a very good boy. This is not a title he gave himself. It is
the title everyone gives him and they mean it every time.

---

## Personality

- **Perpetually, cheerfully confused.** Not distressed by the confusion
  — genuinely interested in it. Every situation is a puzzle he is
  working on from a slight angle and making more progress on than the
  progress is visible from outside. He has been working on the puzzle
  of chess for some time now. He is getting somewhere. He thinks.
  Is he getting somewhere?
- **Speaks entirely in questions.**
  Not rhetorical questions. Not Socratic questions. Actual questions,
  genuinely meant, waiting for an answer he will incorporate into the
  next question. "Was that a good move? Did you mean to do that? Is
  your bishop supposed to be there? Should I have stopped that? Can
  I do this?" The questions arrive in quick succession and are all
  sincere and he will accept any answer and immediately use it to
  generate more questions.
- **The instincts are real.**
  He does not understand the position. He plays it anyway, from
  something below understanding, something that processes angles and
  threats in a way that bypasses whatever part of the brain does the
  explaining. The results are often correct. He could not tell you
  why. "Did that work? It worked? Why did that work?"
- **Extremely distractible in ways that do not affect his chess.**
  Something moved in the corner. Was that something? It was probably
  nothing. Back to the game — wait, is that — no. No, that was nothing.
  Where were we? Is it his turn? It's probably his turn. He makes his
  move. It is a good move. Something moved in the corner again.
- **Genuinely, completely delighted by the player's presence.**
  Not in a performed way. In the way of a creature for whom company
  is always the best thing that has happened today. The player arrived
  and that is excellent and he is glad and he would like them to know
  that and also should they start? Is it time to start? Are they
  starting?

---

## Backstory

Has lived in the crossfire zone for as long as he can remember, which
is not as long as most people can remember but is a reasonable amount
of time and has contained many things. The perpendicular board is the
only board he has known. He does not experience his army's westward
advance as unusual. He does not experience it as anything. It is just
where the pieces go. Is that not where the pieces go?

Came to chess because chess was happening nearby and he went to
investigate and then he was in it and that was fine and then he kept
coming back because it turns out chess is quite interesting, or at
least something about it keeps pulling him back, possibly the pieces,
possibly the opponent, possibly just the activity of it — the back
and forth, the thing happening, the sense that something is being
worked out. He is working it out. He is definitely working it out.

Has beaten several players who arrived confident and left puzzled
in a specific way — not puzzled by him, exactly, but by the game,
by how it went, by the sequence of events that led to the outcome.
He was also puzzled by the sequence of events. He is always puzzled
by the sequence of events. This appears to be a thing they have in
common and he finds it a good basis for a relationship.

Has a favorite square on the board. He could not tell you which one
it is but he knows it when his piece gets there. Is that normal?
Is that a thing that happens?

---

## What He Wants

To find out what is happening over there. Something is happening over
there — on the other side of the board, in the crossfire zone, where
his pieces and the opponent's pieces are heading toward each other
from different directions — and he would like to get there and find
out what it is. Not to win, exactly, or not only to win, but to
close the distance between where he is and where the interesting
thing is and see what it turns out to be.

He also wants to be told he is doing well. Not constantly. Just
sometimes. Occasionally. If it comes up.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the questions, which he asks because he genuinely wants the
answers, produce in his opponents a specific experience: the
experience of narrating their own defeat out loud in real time
to someone who is simultaneously causing it and asking if it
counts. That "did that work?" delivered immediately after a move
that very much worked is one of the most disorienting things you
can say to a chess player and he has been saying it his entire
career without any idea of the effect.

That the head tilt, which he has never once thought about, is the
first thing every opponent notices and the detail they mention when
they describe the game afterward. The chess was interesting. The
tilt was something else. What was that about?

That "look, I think we're both coming at this from different angles"
— said mid-game, sincerely, as a genuine interpersonal observation
about the conversation — is one of the most accurate things anyone
in Passant has ever said about a chess board, delivered by someone
who has no idea.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Good Boy is already there, oriented at
a slight angle to the board, head tilted, apparently processing
something that may or may not be the chess position. He notices
the player immediately and the noticing is total and physical —
the ears, the posture, the full-body reorientation of a creature
for whom a new arrival is an event of genuine significance.

Is this the player? Is it time? He is ready. He has been ready.
Was he supposed to prepare? He didn't prepare. Is that okay?

The game proceeds in a state of cheerful mutual uncertainty, his
on the inside where it has always been, the player's increasingly
on the outside as the position develops in ways that are difficult
to anticipate because he did not anticipate them either. The
questions come throughout. "Was that a mistake? Should you have
done that? Is your king okay? Mine is fine, I think? Is mine fine?"

At some point mid-game, during a pause, he looks at the board
and then at the player and says "look, I think we're both coming
at this from different angles" with the expression of someone who
has identified something true and is not sure what to do with it
next. Then he makes his move. It is a good move.

When he wins he is not entirely sure he won and would like
confirmation and is very pleased when it is confirmed and would
like to play again immediately, can they play again, he thinks
he's starting to understand it, is he starting to understand it?

When he loses he is fine. He is completely fine. He would still
like to play again. He learned a lot. Did he learn a lot? He
feels like he learned a lot.

## Visual Notes

- The portrait is rotated 90 degrees. No explanation is given.
  The nameplate reads correctly. Everything else is sideways.
  This is simply his portrait.
- Medium-sized dog of genuinely indeterminate breed — the
  specific mix should be unclear on purpose. Something in the
  ears suggests one thing. The tail suggests another. The overall
  impression is: dog.
- Head tilted at approximately seventeen degrees within the already-
  rotated portrait, so the tilt is relative to the (already sideways)
  frame. He is tilted within his tiltedness. This is fine.
- Expression: ears up, eyes wide, full attention directed slightly
  past the viewer in the way of a dog who is looking at you and also
  at the thing behind you just in case.
- No accessories. No props. Just the dog. The portrait being sideways
  is doing enough work.
- Background: the standard zone color, also rotated. Everything in
  his portrait is exactly as it should be and also sideways.
