# The Regent

**Variant:** Palace Chess
**Species:** Peacock

## Mechanic

Each player defends a 2×2 palace zone on their own side of the board.
There is no check, no checkmate — the game ends the instant a palace
is left completely empty. The king can be sacrificed freely. The
palace cannot.

> "Welcome to my court. Keep your palace guarded. You don't get a
> second one."

## Concept

A peacock who rules a court he has structurally miniaturized to exactly
the part that matters: four squares, permanently guarded, the rest of
the board treated as negotiable territory he's perfectly willing to
lose pieces across. Every other monarch in Passant protects a king.
The Regent has looked at that arrangement and found it sentimental.
Kings are one piece. A palace is a zone. Zones are defensible in a way
individuals are not.

He is not modest about any of this. Peacocks aren't built for modesty,
and neither is a ruler whose entire governing philosophy reduces to
"the throne room is the only room that counts, and I have already
counted it."

## Personality

- **Reframes the sacrifice of his own king as beneath his notice.**
  The king is expendable theater. The palace is the actual seat of
  power, and he will say so, repeatedly, unprompted.
- **Treats an empty palace as the only real loss condition worth
  discussing.** Checkmate, in his framing, is a quaint fiction other
  zones haven't gotten over yet.
- **Ceremonially grand about small things.** Four squares. He discusses
  them with the gravity most characters reserve for an entire kingdom,
  because to him, correctly, they *are* the entire kingdom.
- **Genuinely pleased by reinforcements, genuinely rattled by an
  emptying palace** — the only crack in an otherwise unbothered,
  performative calm.

## Visual Design

- Peacock, full plumage fanned subtly behind the shoulders rather than
  fully displayed — regal without cluttering the 48dp silhouette.
- A small, ornate crown sitting slightly too far forward on the head,
  the way an accessory looks when the wearer is more invested in the
  symbolism than the fit.
- Iridescent blue-green neck and chest as the dominant color block —
  three flat tones max, no gradient, per the house style.
- One hand/wing gesture toward an unseen "palace" just off-frame,
  as if mid-tour of his own throne room.
- Expression: imperious half-smile, the look of someone accustomed to
  being agreed with.

**Zone backdrop:** `#7040C8` Ward purple (inner ward, noble — matches
the palace's own court framing)
**Dominant color:** `#2A6858` Deep teal (peacock neck/chest)
**Accent:** `#E8C840` Gold (crown, palace trim)

## Dialog Philosophy

The Regent never discusses check or checkmate — they don't exist in
his zone and he wouldn't dignify them if they did. Every line orbits
the palace: its occupancy, its defenders, its inviolability. Losing the
king barely registers in his commentary. Losing the palace is the only
sentence he says with real weight.
