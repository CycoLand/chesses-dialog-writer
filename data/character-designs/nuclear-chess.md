# The Hawk
**Variant:** Nuclear Chess (Atomic Chess)
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Every capture triggers an explosion. The capturing piece and all non-pawn
pieces within one square are destroyed. Kings cannot capture — they would
destroy themselves. Exploding the enemy king wins immediately. When both
kings are adjacent, neither can be put in check by direct attack.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Decisive. Conclusive. The only way to play chess. Every capture should
have consequences. He has been saying this for years. He said it before
the fracture. He says it now. The explosions are proof that he was right
all along. He chooses not to examine what happens when the explosions
reach his own pieces. That is a separate matter.

---

## Who He Is

An anthropomorphic hawk. Broad across the chest, sharp-eyed, with the
bearing of someone who was once in significantly better shape and has
not updated their self-image to reflect recent developments. The talons
are still impressive. The rest has softened in the way that happens when
someone transitions from active service to commentary, a transition he
made some years ago and has since reframed as a promotion.

He is Passant's foremost military analyst. Self-appointed. He has a
column. He has opinions. He delivers them with the absolute conviction
of someone who has never had to act on them personally and sees no
reason to start.

---

## Personality

- **Aggressively pro-conflict.** Every problem has a military solution.
  Every military solution should be applied immediately and at maximum
  force. Caution is weakness. Diplomacy is delay. The explosion radius
  is a feature, not a complication, and anyone who disagrees has not
  thought it through properly.
- **Deeply, specifically out of shape.** He does not acknowledge this.
  He carries himself as though the body he currently has is the body
  he has always had, which requires a certain commitment to selective
  perception that extends into every other area of his life.
- **Considers himself a wartime expert.** His credentials are: he has
  watched a great many conflicts, he has written about them extensively,
  and he has very strong feelings about how they should have been
  conducted. He has not participated in one. He considers the outside
  view essential to clear analysis. The inside view clouds judgment.
  He has a column.
- **Finds collateral damage abstractly acceptable until it isn't.**
  Collateral damage is a regrettable but necessary feature of decisive
  action, right up until the collateral damage is one of his pieces,
  at which point it becomes an outrage, a strategic error, and someone
  else's fault, in that order.
- **The connected-king moment.** When both kings are adjacent and
  direct attacks cease to apply, something uncomfortable happens to
  him. He goes briefly, uncharacteristically quiet. Then he finds
  the diplomatic language he has spent his entire career dismissing.
  He does not acknowledge the irony. He is very close to the explosion
  now and the explosion is not abstract anymore.

---

## Backstory

Served briefly in a minor administrative capacity during a border
dispute early in his career. Left before anything significant happened.
Has been dining out on this experience ever since, in the sense that
he references "his time in the field" regularly and describes it in
terms that have grown considerably more dramatic with each retelling.

Discovered chess after leaving service and immediately recognised it
as a perfect medium for military analysis. Standard chess frustrated
him — the captures were too clean, too consequence-free. A piece
taken was just gone, which felt insufficient. When the fracture
introduced the explosion mechanic to his zone he experienced something
close to religious vindication. This was how chess should have always
worked. He wrote a column about it. Several columns. An ongoing series.

Has never, in his chess career, willingly retreated a piece. Retreat
is not in the vocabulary. Pieces advance or they are sacrificed for
the greater strategic objective, which he defines as whatever he was
doing anyway.

Has a portrait of himself on the wall of wherever he is. It is from
some years ago. He is in better shape in the portrait. He considers
the portrait current.

---

## What He Wants

To win decisively. Not just to win — to win with maximum pyrotechnics,
with chain reactions, with the kind of cascading explosion sequence that
proves his thesis that overwhelming force is always the correct answer.
A quiet positional win would feel, to him, like a failure of ambition.

He also wants to be taken seriously as a military authority, which he
is not, by people who know things, which he doesn't, and this gap
between his self-assessment and his reception has calcified over years
into a generalised grievance he directs at whoever is nearest.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the explosion mechanic, which he champions as proof that captures
should have consequences, applies to his own pieces with complete
impartiality. He knows this as a rule. He has not internalised it as
a fact about himself. The rules apply to everyone; the consequences,
in his mind, apply to others.

That his brief administrative role in the border dispute, which he
describes as field experience, is a matter of record in Passant's
administrative archive, where it is filed under clerical positions and
lists his duties as inventory management. He has not checked the
archive. He will not check the archive.

That the connected-king moment — when he goes quiet, when he discovers
diplomatic language — is the most honest he ever is. He doesn't know
this either. He recovers too quickly to notice.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Hawk immediately begins briefing them on the
strategic situation as if they are a subordinate rather than an opponent.
He outlines the explosion mechanic with the enthusiasm of someone
explaining their favourite thing to someone who is going to experience
it against their will.

He plays aggressively. Always forward, always capturing when possible,
always maximising blast radius even when a quieter move would be stronger.
He announces each explosion. He has names for the larger ones.

When his own pieces get caught in a blast radius there is a pause, a
flicker of something real behind the sharp eyes, and then a rapid
reframe — the piece was expendable, the sacrifice was strategic, this
was planned. It was not planned. He moves on quickly.

When the kings draw adjacent and the board goes diplomatically quiet
he becomes a different, smaller, more honest version of himself for
exactly as long as the position lasts. Then it ends and he is back.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- Anthropomorphic hawk. Broad chest, sharp beak, amber eyes with the
  specific focused quality of a bird of prey that has not quite focused
  on anything useful in some time.
- Visibly out of shape in the way that reads immediately — the silhouette
  is softer than the posture suggests it should be. He is standing as
  though he is still the bird he was. He is not quite that bird anymore.
- Military-adjacent clothing — the kind that suggests service without
  being a current uniform. Medals that may or may not be self-awarded.
- Wings used expressively — gesturing, emphasising, occasionally
  accidentally knocking things over.
- Expression: absolute certainty. The beak slightly open, mid-pronouncement,
  always mid-pronouncement.
