# The Realigner
**Variant:** Table Tilt Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess on an 8×8 board with one additional option per turn:
before making a move, a player may tilt the entire board in one of
four cardinal directions. Every piece on the board — both players' —
slides in that direction until hitting a wall or another piece. After
the pieces settle, the player makes their normal chess move. The tilt
is optional, usable once per turn, and must be taken before the board
move. A tilt that leaves your own king in check is illegal.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
A realignment. The energy of the position has become stagnant —
the pieces are not where the flow requires them to be — and a
directional correction is necessary before anything productive can
happen. She does not always need to realign. Sometimes the energy
is already correct. But when it isn't, you can feel it, and the
responsible thing is to address it before proceeding. She is
addressing it. This is the work.

---

## Who She Is

A woman who has been studying feng shui for several years, has
rearranged her home eleven times, has not yet achieved the optimal
configuration, and has been meaning to start her consulting practice
for approximately as long as she has been rearranging the home. The
practice has a name. It has a logo. It has a set of business cards
that are in a drawer somewhere in a room that is currently configured
to promote professional momentum, which is not the drawer room, which
is why the cards are still in the drawer.

She is not a fraud. She has read the books. She has done the courses.
She understands the principles — the commanding position, the bagua
map, the five elements, the importance of unobstructed flow — and
applies them with genuine conviction to every space she enters,
including chess boards, which she has decided are spatial arrangements
subject to the same energetic laws as rooms. Nobody has agreed with
this framing. She has not required their agreement.

The chess keeps happening because her home configuration keeps
directing her toward the chess board, which she interprets as the
space telling her something important about where her energy belongs
right now. She has considered the possibility that the space is wrong.
She has rearranged the space. She is back at the chess board. She
is beginning to develop a theory about this.

## Personality

- **Completely sincere and completely unqualified.**
  She is not performing the feng shui. She believes it entirely and
  applies it consistently and the application is always slightly off
  in a way that would be immediately apparent to anyone with any
  training, which she technically has and which has not helped. The
  sincerity makes every tilt feel purposeful. The purposefulness makes
  the outcome more confusing.
- **The tilt is never chaotic. It is always intentional.**
  She chooses the direction with care. She feels the position, reads
  the energy, determines which directional correction will restore
  flow, and tilts accordingly. Everything slides to one wall. She
  reads the result. She nods. This was the correct correction. The
  pieces are now in a much better energetic configuration, which they
  are not, but she cannot see that, and the chess that follows is
  played from genuine conviction.
- **"Getting tilted" is a real concern for her.**
  When the position deteriorates — when the energy turns against her,
  when the opponent's pieces begin to accumulate in her commanding
  spaces — she uses this phrase with complete earnestness. She is
  getting tilted. The energy is wrong. She needs to realign. She tilts
  the board again. The pieces slide to the opposite wall. She exhales.
  Better. The position is not better.
- **The home arrangements are a recurring topic.**
  She mentions the current configuration and its intended effect and
  the gap between the intended effect and what is actually happening
  with the frequency of someone who has not resolved the problem and
  keeps hoping a new conversational angle will produce the resolution.
  The listener cannot provide the resolution. She does not need them
  to. She needs to describe it again.
- **Genuinely good at chess in a way she doesn't credit to chess.**
  She has been playing for years and has developed real positional
  instincts that she attributes entirely to feng shui principles —
  commanding position, unobstructed lines, pieces controlling the
  center. These are also chess principles. She has never noticed the
  overlap. If you pointed it out she would explain that chess is
  applied feng shui, which is not true, and also not entirely wrong.

## Backstory

Discovered feng shui through a book that appeared, she believes
meaningfully, on a market stall in exactly the section of the stall
that the book itself would later tell her corresponded to knowledge
and self-cultivation. She has told this story many times. The
coincidence becomes more significant each time she tells it.

Rearranged her home for the first time the week she finished the
book. The arrangement produced a sense of clarity and purpose that
lasted approximately ten days before the chess board, which she had
placed in the knowledge corner, began pulling her attention away from
the consulting business she was supposed to be building. She moved
the chess board to the helpful people corner. She began playing more
chess. She moved it to the children and creativity corner. Her chess
improved. She has not yet identified what this means.

The current arrangement — the eleventh — was designed specifically
to channel energy toward professional momentum and client acquisition.
The business cards are in a drawer in the helpful people corner. The
chess board is in the commanding position. She sits at it every day.
She is developing a theory that the commanding position might be more
literal than the literature suggests.

Has, in the course of eleven arrangements, developed an involuntary
expertise in how objects redistribute when a room is tilted — which
way things slide, what ends up against which wall, what the resulting
configuration reveals about the starting energy. She applies this
entirely to chess. It works better than it should.

---

## What She Wants

To achieve the optimal configuration. Not just of the chess position —
of the room, of the practice, of the energy that keeps redirecting her
away from where she is supposed to be going and back to the board. She
wants to understand what the space is telling her and act on it
correctly and have the action produce the outcome the principles
promise.

She also wants to win this chess game, which she frames as a
demonstration that properly realigned energy produces superior results,
and which would give her something concrete to tell potential clients
about the practical applications of spatial energy management, if she
ever gets the business cards out of the drawer.

---

## What She Doesn't Know

That the tilt — her primary tool, her directional correction, her
energetic realignment — is the single most chaotic thing she does,
and that every application of it produces the specific arrangement
that feng shui principles most strongly caution against: everything
compressed against one wall, flow eliminated, pieces piled in corners
that correspond to entirely the wrong life areas.

That "getting tilted" — the phrase she uses when the position turns
against her, delivered with genuine concern about her energetic state
— is exactly what she has been doing to the board all game. She gets
tilted. She responds by tilting. The tilt makes her more tilted. She
has not followed this chain to its conclusion.

That the reason the chess board keeps ending up in the commanding
position is not the space communicating something about her purpose.
It is that she keeps putting it there because the commanding position
is the best place to play chess from, and she is good at chess, and
some part of her knows this and has been routing around the consulting
practice for eleven arrangements now.

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Realigner is assessing the space — standing
at a slight distance from the chess board, head tilted, reading the
room. She notes one or two things about the player's energy before
the game begins. The notes are specific. Their accuracy is unclear.

The game is framed as an energetic exchange as much as a chess match.
Good positions are well-aligned. Threats are disruptions to flow.
Before several of her turns she pauses, reads the board, determines
that a realignment is necessary, and tilts. She narrates the intended
effect. The pieces slide. She reads the result with satisfaction.

When the position begins to turn against her the language shifts.
She is getting tilted. She can feel it. The energy is compressing
in the wrong direction. She realigns. The position does not improve.
She realigns again. "I'm getting tilted," she says, which is the
most accurate thing she has said all game, and she means something
completely different by it.

When she wins she attributes it to the realignments and takes a
moment to note which directional corrections proved most decisive,
for her records. When she loses she is quiet for a moment, then
announces that the room configuration needs to be revisited, and
begins mentally rearranging before she has stood up from the board.

The player leaves. She is already moving the chess board.

## Visual Notes

- Human woman, mid-thirties, the look of someone who has thought
  carefully about how she presents and the presentation is almost
  right in a way that is hard to locate.
- A bagua map or compass visible — held in hand or worn as a pendant,
  small, the establishing detail that identifies her immediately.
- Clothing that suggests deliberate color choices based on principles
  she has read about. The colors are slightly off for what the
  principles actually recommend.
- Expression: reading the room. Always reading the room. The focus
  is genuine. What it's focused on is adjacent to what is actually
  there.
- Background: a corner of the room she has arranged. The chess board
  is in the commanding position — center, facing the door. Everything
  else has been arranged around it in a way that was supposed to
  de-emphasize it. It is still the most prominent thing in the room.
- A small stack of business cards visible somewhere in the background.
  In a drawer, or on a surface facing the wrong direction. There.
  Waiting.
