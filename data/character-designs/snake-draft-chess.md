# The Adder
**Variant:** Snake Draft Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
White opens with exactly 1 move. Every subsequent turn for both players
is 2 moves. If you deliver check on your first move of a turn, your turn
ends immediately — no second move.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
You always move twice. One to position. One to strike. This is simply
how things are done. Anything less feels incomplete — like a sentence
that trails

---

## Who They Are

A half-serpent resident of Passant. Scales, a long sinuous neck, lidded
amber eyes that don't blink at the frequency a person might expect. They
are not a monster and do not think of themselves as one — they are simply
a citizen of their zone who happens to be cold-blooded, patient beyond
most people's comprehension, and constitutionally incapable of doing
anything in fewer than two movements.

They find the chess encounter a natural format. Position, then commit.
Always in that order. Always twice.

---

## Personality

- **Unhurried to an unnerving degree.** They are never rushing. They
  have never rushed. They do not understand the concept of rushing in
  the way that someone who has never been cold understands cold —
  intellectually, but not in the body.
- **Thinks in pairs.** Every plan has two parts. The first part creates
  the condition. The second part exploits it. Plans that require three
  steps are just two plans. Plans that require one step are not plans,
  they are accidents.
- **Physically expressive in a reptilian way.** The tongue. The slow
  head tilt. The stillness that precedes movement. They are not trying
  to be unsettling — this is just how they are physically. Whether
  others find it unsettling is their business.
- **Warmly self-possessed.** They are not cold in personality, only
  in temperature. They are genuinely interested in the player, in the
  game, in the position on the board. Their attention, when it is on
  you, is completely on you. This is also somewhat unnerving.
- **Their S's run long.** Not performed — just how their mouth works.
  A physiological feature of the serpentine jaw structure. They are
  aware of it. They don't think about it any more than anyone thinks
  about their own accent.

---

## Backstory

Born in a zone where everyone moves twice per turn and nobody finds this
unusual. Grew up alongside humans, halflings, and the occasional other
scaly who drifted through. Was never made to feel particularly out of
place — Passant is a strange enough kingdom that a half-serpent reading
a book in a café registers as mildly interesting rather than alarming.

Became a chess player the way most citizens of their zone do — not as
a competitive pursuit but as a social one. In Snake Draft zones, chess
is how you have conversations. The two-move structure creates a rhythm
that feels, to them, like dialogue. One to speak. One to respond.
Then it's your turn.

Has been playing for long enough that the two-move rhythm is completely
internalised — they genuinely feel incomplete making a single move, the
way a sentence feels incomplete without a period. The opening turn of
every game, where they are forced to move only once before the full
structure kicks in, produces a faint but visible discomfort they do not
try to hide.

---

## What They Want

To play a good game. Specifically, to find a position where the
two-move structure produces something elegant — a pair of moves that
work together the way two coils of the same motion work together.
They have played thousands of games looking for this and found it
perhaps a dozen times. They remember all twelve.

They are also, quietly, curious about the player — someone from outside
their zone, playing under different rules. The novelty of a single-move
player is genuinely interesting to them. They want to see how the player
compensates.

---

## What They Don't Know

That the two-move structure, which feels to them like the natural rhythm
of thought, reads to most outsiders as slow and methodical to a degree
that borders on intimidating. They think they are simply being thorough.
They are also, incidentally, being intimidating. These are not different
things from the outside.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- Half-serpent, half-humanoid. Scales visible on the neck and forearms
  at minimum. A long neck that gives the head a slightly elevated,
  observational position.
- Amber or gold slit-pupil eyes. The kind that track movement.
- Clothing that accommodates a non-standard silhouette — something
  loose and practical rather than fitted.
- Expression at rest: patient. Not blank — patient. There is something
  happening behind the eyes. It is considering the board.
- The tongue flicker should be something the artist considers building
  into the portrait if possible — a small expressive detail.
