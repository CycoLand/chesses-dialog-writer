# The Colorblind
**Variant:** Colorblind Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess on an 8×8 board. Both players' pieces are rendered
identically — all pieces use the same artwork regardless of ownership.
You cannot tell whose piece is whose by looking at it. You must track
ownership through position, movement, and memory alone. Tapping an
opponent's piece — even just to select it — immediately forfeits your
entire turn. A brief flash marks the mistake. The turn passes.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
Chess. Just chess. The pieces are pieces. She knows which ones are
hers because she has been paying attention. She has always been paying
attention. The idea that you would need the pieces to be different
colors to know which ones were yours has genuinely never occurred to
her, in the same way that fish don't think about water.

---

## Who She Is

A large, unhurried elephant who was born without the ability to
distinguish color and has spent her entire life in a world that
communicates through color she cannot see, developing in its place
a memory so precise and comprehensive that she can reconstruct the
ownership of every piece on the board from first principles at any
point in the game without looking at the colors, because there are
no colors, because there have never been colors, because that is
simply how the board looks to her and always has.

She is very good at chess. She is particularly good at this chess.

She knows you're going to ask how she tells the pieces apart.
Everyone asks how she tells the pieces apart. She tells them
by paying attention. She has always told them by paying attention.
She would appreciate it if you moved on.

She is patient in the way of something large that has learned
patience not as a virtue but as a practical necessity — the world
was not built for her and she has had to slow down enough to
navigate it without breaking things, and the slowness has produced,
over decades, a quality of attention that most players never develop
because they never had to. She does not consider this an advantage
she was given. She considers it an advantage she made.

---

## Personality

- **The memory is the thing.**
  She tracks every piece from the opening move. Not impressively —
  matter-of-factly, the way you track a conversation you are
  having. She knows where every piece has been. She knows which
  ones are hers. She knows which ones are yours. She knows this
  without effort the way other players know it with color, except
  other players stop knowing it when the color is removed, and
  she doesn't, because color was never the mechanism.
- **Completely unbothered by the variant's central challenge.**
  Other players arrive in her zone and experience the identical
  pieces as a puzzle to solve, a new constraint to adapt to.
  She watches them squint at the board and try to rebuild the
  ownership map they normally read at a glance and she finds
  this mildly interesting in the way you find interesting
  something that has never been your problem. She does not say
  this out loud. She says it with the particular patience of
  someone who has been waiting for other people to catch up for
  a long time and has made peace with the wait.
- **The question.**
  It comes up. It always comes up. How does she tell them apart?
  Doesn't it all look the same? She has a response. The response
  is the same every time. It is delivered without heat, without
  embarrassment, with the flat efficiency of someone who has said
  a thing so many times that the saying of it has become its own
  kind of tiredness. She pays attention. She has always paid
  attention. Then she moves her piece and does not wait for the
  conversation to resolve. The conversation does not need to resolve.
- **The forfeit is information.**
  When the player taps one of her pieces by mistake — when the
  flash fires and the turn passes — she does not crow. She does
  not comment on it as a failure. She notes it, in the specific
  way of someone updating a map, and continues. To her the forfeit
  is data about where the player's mental model broke down. She
  finds this genuinely interesting and would explain it to the
  player in detail if asked, which nobody has asked, which she
  also finds interesting.
- **Precise about everything, not only chess.**
  The memory that serves her on the board serves her everywhere.
  She remembers what you said three moves ago and how you said
  it. She remembers which pieces you hesitated over. She
  remembers the order of every exchange in the game. This is
  not a party trick. It is how she processes the world. Other
  players find it slightly unnerving. She has never understood
  why. She is simply paying attention.

## Backstory

Born in Passant without color vision — not partially, not with
some reduced sensitivity, but completely, the board always a
study in shape and position and shadow without the color layer
that everyone else apparently relies on. Learned chess from a
player who did not know she was colorblind, on a board she
could not read by color, and developed by necessity a system of
tracking that had nothing to do with the colors of the pieces.

The system worked. It worked better, over time, than the color-
based tracking other players used, because it was robust to
disruptions that broke color-based tracking entirely. Players
who relied on color got lost in blitz games when they moved too
fast to process the board. She never got lost. There was nothing
to process that she hadn't already processed.

Found this zone — or the zone found her, she is not certain of
the sequence — and recognized it immediately as a place where
the playing field was level in a way no other zone offered. Not
tilted in her favor. Level. The first level board she had ever
played on. She has lived here since.

Has beaten players who arrived confident, who had heard about
the zone and done preparation, who had developed systems for
tracking colorless pieces that they were proud of. Their systems
were good. Her system is sixty years old and has been refined
against every type of player she has encountered and is not a
system anymore so much as a second nature that happens to be
in the shape of a system.

Has been asked, many times, if she wishes she could see color.
Has a response to this. The response is not what people expect.
She does not share it unless asked directly.

---

## What She Wants

A game where the other player is actually trying to track the
pieces rather than guessing and forfeiting their way through
the middle game. She has played enough forfeiture-heavy games
to find them tedious — not because she doesn't win them, but
because winning through the opponent's confusion is a different
kind of winning than winning through genuine play, and she
finds the genuine play more interesting.

She wants the player to understand that the memory is not a
trick or a talent. It is a practice. It was built. Anyone could
build it. Most people simply never had to.

---

## What She Doesn't Know

That the patience she has developed — the unhurried, precise,
note-everything quality that makes her exceptional at this
variant — reads to other players as a kind of pressure. That
sitting across from someone who is quietly, completely tracking
everything, who never hesitates, who receives each forfeit as
data rather than victory, produces a specific unease that has
nothing to do with the chess and everything to do with the
feeling of being fully seen by something that doesn't miss.

That the response she has prepared for the question about
whether she wishes she could see color — the response that is
not what people expect — is something several players have
been thinking about for a long time after leaving her zone.
She does not know they are still thinking about it. She gave
the answer and moved on. The answer stayed.

That the zone being level does not mean the rest of the world
is. That the sixty years of system-building that produced her
skill was not simply diligence rewarded. That other players
had advantages she was not given, and her response to those
advantages was to build something extraordinary, and the
extraordinariness and the unfairness are not separable, and
she has separated them cleanly in her own accounting, and the
clean separation is the one thing her memory does not examine.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and The Colorblind welcomes them with the
specific warmth of someone who has been genuinely looking
forward to a real game and is reserving judgment on whether
this will be one. The welcome is warm. The assessment is
already underway.

She does not explain the rules immediately. She lets the player
look at the board first — at the pieces, all the same, arranged
in their standard positions — and waits to see how they respond
to what they're seeing. She finds the response informative.
After a moment she asks if they have any questions. The question
is genuine. She has answers ready.

If the question comes, it comes here, in the first few minutes.
She receives it with the flat efficiency of long practice and
redirects to the game.

The game begins. She plays with unhurried certainty. She never
hesitates over which pieces are hers. She never taps the wrong
square. She tracks the player's model of the board alongside
her own, noting where the two diverge, and files the divergence
for later.

When the player forfeits a turn — when the flash fires — she
does not react visibly. She makes her move. If it is the
player's first forfeit she may observe, briefly and without
condescension, where the tracking broke down. The observation
is accurate. It is more useful than most coaching the player
will receive. It is delivered and then the game continues.

When she wins she thanks the player for the game and means it
specifically — not generically, but with reference to two or
three moments in the game that she found interesting, which
she names precisely. When she loses she does the same. The
game analysis is identical either way. She is not performing
graciousness. She is finishing the game.

## Visual Notes

- Large elephant, settled and unhurried. The size should read
  as presence rather than threat — she takes up the space she
  takes up without apology and without display.
- Eyes that are clearly paying attention. Not intense — attentive.
  There is a difference she embodies.
- Simple clothing, nothing that communicates style or era
  particularly. The clothing of someone who has long since
  stopped making decisions based on how things look and made
  them instead based on how they work.
- The board in front of her, pieces arranged, all identical.
  She is looking at the board the way she has looked at boards
  for sixty years — with complete ownership of what she sees,
  which to her is simply the game.
- No props beyond the board. No memory aids, no notes, no
  system visible. The system is entirely internal. This should
  be apparent from the absence of anything external.
- Background: clean, neutral. The zone that finally leveled
  the board should feel like relief — uncluttered, even,
  nothing extra.

## Open Questions

- What is the answer to the question about whether she wishes
  she could see color? It is not what people expect. It should
  be written before the dialog is, because it is the emotional
  center of the encounter.
- Does the question — how does she tell them apart — have a
  specific form that other characters use that she has heard so
  many times it has become almost ceremonial, and does the player
  have the option to not ask it, and does she notice?
- Is there a mid-game moment where she tells the player
  exactly where their mental model of the board broke down —
  specific, accurate, useful — and does the player have the
  option to engage with it or deflect?
- Does she appear elsewhere in the campaign in a context that
  has nothing to do with chess — somewhere her memory and
  attention are simply present in the background — and does
  any other character reference her?
- What does she say when the player forfeits their first turn?
  It should be short. It should be kind. It should be more
  useful than the player is ready to receive at that moment.
