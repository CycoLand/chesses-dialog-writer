# The Colony
**Variant:** Horde Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
One side fields a standard 16-piece army. The other fields 36 pawns
and nothing else — no king, no back rank pieces, just pawns. The
standard army wins by capturing every single pawn. The horde wins by
checkmating the king. The horde has no king to protect. Individual
losses are irrelevant. There are always more.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
The colony advances. Individual units are expended as required. The
queen does not concern herself with individual units. The queen
concerns herself with the board.

---

## Who They Are

A colony of ants. Tens of thousands of them, operating as a single
entity, occupying the space across the chess board from the player.
The individual ants are the pawns — each one a worker, each one
expendable, each one replaceable within the colony's accounting by
a margin too small to register. The colony does not have a face.
The colony does not need one.

The queen is somewhere in the mass. She is larger than the others.
She does not move unless the colony moves. She is the voice — the
only voice, the only one whose communications are intended for
external consumption. When the colony speaks, it is the queen
speaking. The workers carry her words to the surface in the form
of vibrations, pheromones, and occasionally spelling things out
with their bodies if the queen determines that the situation
requires emphasis.

She is not cruel. She is not warm. She is completely, utterly
practical in the way of something that has been optimizing the
same set of objectives for millions of years and has no remaining
capacity for anything that doesn't serve the colony.

---

## Personality — The Queen

- **Speaks in the collective first person.** We. Us. The colony.
  Never I, never me. The distinction between the queen and the
  colony is not one she makes or acknowledges. She is the colony.
  The colony is her. The grammar reflects this.
- **Completely unbothered by individual losses.** A captured pawn
  is a worker expended in service of the advance. The queen
  acknowledges it the way you acknowledge a footstep — it happened,
  it was part of moving forward, the next step is already occurring.
  There is no grief. There is no hesitation. There is only the
  next advance.
- **Ancient and patient in a way that makes the chess game feel
  very small.** The colony has been doing this longer than Passant
  has existed. The queen has seen things that would reframe every
  other character in the campaign as a brief and recent development.
  She finds the player interesting the way a geologist finds a
  new rock formation interesting — notable, worth examining,
  ultimately just another thing the earth produced.
- **Tactically precise underneath the apparent mindlessness.**
  36 pawns moving in coordinated waves is not mindless — it is
  a different kind of mind, one that operates through distributed
  intelligence rather than centralized planning. The queen does
  not calculate the way a chess player calculates. She reads
  the board chemically, structurally, through the colony's
  collective sense of where the pressure is and where it isn't.
  The result is chess played from a completely alien angle that
  is surprisingly effective.
- **The workers are an extension of her body.** When a pawn
  moves, the queen moved it. When a pawn is captured, a part
  of her was taken. She does not express this as loss. She
  expresses it as adjustment. The colony adjusts. The colony
  continues.

## Backstory

The colony predates Passant. This is not a claim — it is simply
true, and the queen would confirm it without particular emphasis
if asked, the way you confirm your own age. The colony was here
before the kingdom, before the fracture, before chess, before
anyone thought to put a board between two parties and call it
resolution.

Chess arrived in their territory at some point — the queen does
not track time in years — and the colony adapted to it the way
the colony adapts to everything: by incorporating it into the
advance. The pawns became the board pieces. The board became
a terrain to be crossed. The win condition — checkmate the
king — became an objective to be reached through attrition
and overwhelming presence.

The colony has played many games. The queen does not remember
them individually. She remembers patterns — the shape of a
position that preceded a win, the configuration that preceded
a loss, the pheromone signature of a board that is about to
tip one way or another. The individual games dissolved into
aggregate knowledge long ago.

The colony lives beneath the zone's surface. The chess encounter
happens at the entrance to the colony — a clearing where the
ants emerge, arrange themselves, and wait. The board is already
set up when the player arrives. The queen surfaces when the
player sits down. The workers have been there for some time.

---

## What She Wants

The advance. Not victory in the abstract — the physical advance
of the colony across the board, pawn by pawn, rank by rank, until
the king is cornered. The win condition is a human concept imposed
on something the colony was already doing. The colony was always
going to advance. Chess just gave it a frame.

She also, somewhere in the ancient distributed intelligence of
the colony, finds the player interesting. Not as an opponent —
as a phenomenon. A single entity, making all the decisions alone,
with no colony to distribute the load. She has encountered this
before. She finds it both impressive and structurally fragile.

---

## What She Doesn't Know

Nothing about Passant's politics, history, or the other characters
in the campaign. These are not relevant to the advance. She is
aware that a fracture occurred. She noted it as a change in
conditions and adapted. The specific cause and the specific
parties involved are not data the colony requires.

That the player is not, from the colony's perspective, the real
threat — the real threat is always attrition, always the slow
reduction of the 36, always the possibility of the last pawn
being taken. She has come close to that outcome before. The
colony does not speak of those games. The pheromone signature
of those positions is still in the collective memory and produces
a specific, nameless response in every worker who encounters it.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives at the clearing. The ants are already there —
have been there, arranged around the board in a stillness that
is not stillness but readiness. The queen surfaces as the player
sits. She is larger than the others. She does not look at the
player directly. She looks at the board.

The game begins. The pawns advance in waves — not randomly, not
uniformly, but in a pattern that responds to the player's position
the way a fluid responds to an obstacle. The queen narrates nothing.
She comments occasionally, in the collective first person, on the
state of the board. The comments are accurate and impersonal.

When a pawn is captured the workers around it close the gap
immediately. There is no pause. There is no acknowledgment beyond
a single line from the queen that notes the adjustment and
continues. The advance continues.

When the colony wins, the queen notes it. The workers begin
to disperse back into the colony. The board is disassembled
by hundreds of small bodies working in parallel. The queen
descends. The clearing is empty in minutes.

When the colony loses — when the last pawn is taken — there
is a long silence. The workers are still. The queen is still.
Then she descends without a word and the workers follow and
the clearing is empty and it is unclear whether the colony
will return or has simply filed this outcome in the aggregate
and moved on. It has moved on. The colony always moves on.

## Visual Notes (for later)

- The portrait should be the queen, but the queen should not
  be alone in it — workers visible around her, on her, part
  of her silhouette. The boundary between the queen and the
  colony is not a clean line.
- She is larger than the workers but not dramatically so —
  larger in the way of authority rather than size. The
  difference is visible. It is not enormous.
- The background of the portrait should suggest the colony
  beneath — the surface of the clearing, and beneath it the
  hint of tunnels, of depth, of something much larger than
  what is visible.
- Expression: the queen does not have a human expression.
  She has an orientation — toward the board, toward the
  objective, toward the advance. That orientation should
  read in the portrait as clearly as an expression would.
- The encounter UI should show the 36 pawns as a visual
  mass — the horde should look like a horde, not like
  a standard piece arrangement.
