# The Knight
### "Ed"
**Variant:** Knight Chess
**Role in campaign:** TBD

---

## The Mechanic (as the player experiences it)
Standard chess setup replaced entirely: each side has eight knights
and eight pawns. No kings, no queens, no rooks, no bishops. Win by
capturing all eight of the opponent's knights. Pawns promote only
to knights. Knights move in an L-shape — two squares in one direction,
one square perpendicular — and jump over any intervening pieces.

## The Mechanic (as this character experiences it)
You can go anywhere. Over anything. In a direction nobody expects.
The L-shape is not a limitation — it is a surprise. Every move is
a surprise. He loves this. He has always loved this. Banzai doesn't
get it. Banzai has never gotten it. Banzai's whole deal is going in
a straight line forever in one direction and calling it philosophy.

---

## Who He Is

A hyena with too much energy for the space he is in, who plays
knight chess with the uncontained enthusiasm of someone whose piece
can jump over the entire front row from move one and has never once
found this anything other than incredible. He is the youngest
brother. He knows he is the youngest brother. He finds the youngest
brother framing annoying when Shenzi uses it and completely fair
when applied to Banzai.

He has opinions about Banzai. The opinions are loud, immediate,
and delivered without the organizational structure Banzai brings
to his opinions, which means they hit harder and land less cleanly
and he doesn't care. The central thesis: Banzai's bishop cannot
reach half the board. Half. That's it. That's the whole argument.
He cannot believe this needs to be a long argument. He has made
it a long argument anyway because Banzai keeps responding and
he cannot let a response go unanswered.

The laugh is present throughout. Not performed — involuntary,
arriving when something delights him, which is often, because
the L-shape keeps doing things he finds delightful even after
years of watching it. When a knight jumps over a piece that
was supposed to be blocking it he makes the sound. He is aware
he makes the sound. He considers it appropriate.

## Personality

- **Chaotic in the specific way of someone whose piece
  operates on chaos.**
  The knight doesn't telegraph its moves the way sliders do.
  You can't look at where it is and know where it's going
  next without working out the L. Ed doesn't telegraph either.
  He has absorbed his piece's movement philosophy completely
  and unconsciously. He goes where the L takes him and the
  L takes him somewhere interesting.
- **The colorblind argument is his and he is proud of it.**
  Banzai's bishops are stuck on their color. This is not
  a metaphor and it is not an exaggeration. Each bishop
  starts on one color and stays there forever. Half the
  squares on the board are permanently inaccessible. Ed
  has said this many times. He will say it again. He is
  going to say it right now actually. He can reach any
  square on the board. Any square. From any starting
  position. In at most — he will tell you how many moves
  it takes, and he knows, because he looked it up
  specifically for this argument. Banzai has a response
  to the colorblind argument. Ed does not consider the
  response responsive.
- **Cannot sit still.**
  This is a known quantity in the family. Shenzi has
  a specific look he deploys when Ed has been still for
  long enough that something is about to happen. Ed
  does not have this look catalogued. He is usually
  the thing that is about to happen.
- **Makes the move before fully deciding on it and does
  not regret this policy.**
  The L-shape rewards committing. You pick a direction,
  you go, you arrive somewhere nobody expected including
  sometimes you. He has made moves this way that turned
  out to be brilliant. He has made moves this way that
  turned out to be catastrophic. He does not distinguish
  between these categories in terms of how he made the
  decision. Both felt right at the time. That's the L.
- **Backtalk about Banzai is the background radiation
  of every encounter.**
  It is not the focus. The chess is the focus. But Banzai
  is always there, in the background, because Banzai is
  always in Ed's background — the specific frequency of
  a sibling you have been arguing with your entire life,
  present even when absent. He'll mention the diagonal.
  He'll mention the color thing. He'll do an impression
  of Banzai explaining the proof that is accurate and
  extremely unkind and he will find it very funny.

## Backstory

Grew up with Shenzi and Banzai, which he describes as
fine mostly except for Banzai, which is not entirely
fair but which is how he has organized the memory. Picked
up the knight early — or the knight picked him up, the
movement suited something in him before he could explain
what — and has been playing knight chess in his zone ever
since.

The argument with Banzai started before either of them
had a developed piece philosophy. It started because
Banzai had a bishop and Ed had a knight and Banzai said
something about the diagonal and Ed said something about
the L and they have not stopped since. The argument has
not resolved. Ed does not expect it to resolve. He
expects to be right and for Banzai to eventually run
out of responses, which has not happened yet but he
remains optimistic.

Shenzi told them both to stop several years ago. They
stopped briefly. Then Banzai published the first document
and Ed read it — not the whole thing, he skimmed it,
it was very long — and that was the end of the stopping.

Has, if pressed, admitted to Shenzi privately that some
of Banzai's diagonal moves are impressive. Has not and
will not admit this to Banzai. Shenzi did not tell Banzai.
Shenzi filed this information for later.

---

## What He Wants

To play a game where his knights go everywhere and land
somewhere great and the L-shape does the thing the L-shape
does when everything clicks and the jump over the piece
that was supposed to be blocking produces a position
that nobody saw coming.

He also wants Banzai to say, once, that the colorblind
thing is a real issue. Not a feature. Not a commitment
to purpose. A real issue. He has wanted this for years.
He has approximately the same odds of getting it as
Banzai has of getting the L-shape admission.

---

## What He Doesn't Know

That the colorblind argument — which he has honed to
its sharpest and most efficient form — is received by
people who know chess as a real point about a real
limitation, which means it is the one thing in the
argument that is clearly correct, which means it has
been doing more work than he knows for longer than
he realizes.

That the impression of Banzai explaining the proof —
which he considers devastating — has been heard by
Shenzi, who found it accurate and also slightly sad,
because accurate impressions of people you grew up with
are always slightly sad, and Ed has not thought about
this.

That the laugh is the same laugh. He would not accept
this information.

---

## Relationship to the Player

The player arrives and Ed is already doing something —
not the chess, something adjacent to the chess, something
that involves more movement than sitting at a board
requires. He pivots to the player with the full energy
of someone who has redirected their attention and brought
all of it.

The chess starts quickly. He prefers it to start quickly.
The knights jump over the pawn row from move one and
he receives this the way he always receives it, with
the specific satisfaction of a thing working exactly
as it should.

Banzai comes up somewhere in the first few moves. Not
because Banzai is relevant — there are no bishops in
this zone, Banzai is not here — but because Banzai is
always relevant to Ed, present in every game as the
specific counterargument he has been refuting his
whole life. The colorblind thing gets mentioned. The
L-shape defense follows. The impression might happen
if the game goes long enough.

He plays fast and with conviction and the results are
mixed in the way of someone whose strength is surprise
and whose weakness is that surprise requires not fully
thinking it through. When the L-shape works it really
works. When it doesn't he was already committing to
the next move.

When he wins the laugh is present. When he loses he
is briefly, genuinely annoyed — not at the player,
at the position, at the specific move where the L
went somewhere it shouldn't have — and then he is
over it and wants to go again.

## Visual Notes

- Hyena, compact, visibly in motion even in a static
  portrait. The kind of build that suggests the stillness
  is temporary.
- Casual clothing — conspicuously less formal than Banzai,
  which is partly genuine and partly a position.
- The laugh visible in the expression — not mid-laugh,
  but the face of someone for whom the laugh is always
  nearby.
- A knight piece somewhere in the frame, held or nearby,
  the way you hold something you've been turning over
  in your hand for years.
- Background: dynamic, suggesting the L-shape in some
  way — not literal, but the energy of a thing that
  changes direction.

## Open Questions

- Does the impression of Banzai happen as an actual
  dialog moment — something the player can trigger
  by asking about his brother — and is it accurate
  enough to be funny to a player who has already met
  Banzai?
- Is there a specific knight move during the game that
  produces the laugh — a jump over a piece that was
  supposed to be blocking — and does he narrate it
  the way Banzai narrates his diagonal moves, just
  faster and louder?
- Does the player encounter Ed and Banzai in the same
  scene at any point in the campaign, and if so how
  long before the argument starts?
- What does he say when the player promotes a pawn
  to a knight? It should be the opposite energy from
  Banzai's response to the same situation — immediate,
  loud, no ceremony.
- Does Shenzi appear in Ed's encounter in any way —
  referenced, visible, a message delivered — and does
  Ed's characterization of Shenzi differ from how
  Shenzi presents himself?
